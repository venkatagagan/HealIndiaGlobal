# Chandigarh city guide

Chandigarh is a planned city at the foot of the Shivalik hills. Together with Mohali and Panchkula it forms the "tricity", and hospitals are spread across all three. The city is laid out in numbered sectors, which makes addresses easy to follow. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | Chandigarh is a Union Territory and the capital of both Punjab and Haryana. Mohali is in Punjab and Panchkula in Haryana |
| Main airport | Chandigarh International Airport (IXC), officially Shaheed Bhagat Singh International Airport, on the Mohali side |
| Local language(s) | Hindi and Punjabi. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |
| Time zone | India Standard Time (UTC+5:30) |
| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Chandigarh's airport has only a limited number of international flights. Many travellers fly into Delhi and continue by domestic flight, by train or by road. The train or road journey takes several hours. From Chandigarh airport, allow roughly 20 to 45 minutes by road to most parts of the tricity.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. If you arrive in Delhi, ask your coordinator about a comfortable car for the longer transfer. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Trains from Delhi.** Trains arrive at Chandigarh railway station. They suit you only if you are walking comfortably, and you should arrange help with your luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** International routes into Chandigarh change, so check your current options. If you travel via Delhi, check connecting flight, train and road options and journey times.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | December–January | Cold, with chilly nights and foggy mornings. Fog can delay flights and trains |
| Spring | February–March | Mild and pleasant, with gardens in bloom |
| Summer | April–June | Hot and dry. Temperatures can exceed 40°C |
| Monsoon | July–September | Humid, with heavy showers and occasional waterlogging |
| Post-monsoon | October–November | Pleasant days, but air quality can worsen, partly because of smoke from crop-residue burning in the region |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Air quality.** From around October to January, check the daily Air Quality Index (AQI). If you have a heart or lung condition, or have had chest or heart surgery, ask your doctor whether you should wear a mask (such as an N95) and limit time outdoors.
- **Fog.** If you fly or take a train in December or January, leave a buffer in your plans for delays.
- **Heat.** In summer, stay indoors during the hottest hours and drink fluids as your care team advises.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.

## Getting around

- **App-based cabs and auto-rickshaws.** Cabs are the most practical option for most patients. For autos, agree the fare before you set off. Autos are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Buses.** CTU city buses link the tricity but can be crowded, so they are not suitable while you are recovering. There is no metro.
- **Traffic.** Traffic is lighter than in the largest cities but busy at peak times, including between Chandigarh, Mohali and Panchkula.
- **Walking.** The city has many green belts, but pavements vary and large roundabouts can be hard to cross on foot. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Sukhna Lake.** A flat lakeside promenade near the hills, popular in the early morning and evening. Boating is available.
- **Zakir Hussain Rose Garden, Sector 16.** A large rose garden with flat paths. The Rose Festival, usually in February, draws crowds.
- **Government Museum and Art Gallery, Sector 10.** Indoor galleries that are usually quiet.
- **Leisure Valley.** A long chain of gardens running through the city, with flat paths.
- **Capitol Complex.** Buildings designed by Le Corbusier and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Visits are by guided tour, and you will need ID.
- **Rock Garden.** Sculptures made from recycled materials by Nek Chand. It has narrow passages, low arches and uneven, sometimes steep paths, so save it for later in your recovery.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave hill trips to places such as Kasauli or Shimla, with their winding mountain roads and higher altitude, until later in your recovery, and go only with your doctor's agreement.

## Food

Punjabi dishes include:

- chole bhature
- rajma chawal
- makki di roti with sarson da saag, in winter
- paneer and tandoori dishes, and butter chicken
- lassi

Roadside dhabas are popular. The food is often rich and buttery. Vegetarian food is everywhere, halal food is available in some restaurants, and Jain meals can usually be prepared on request.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Current international routes into Chandigarh International Airport
- Connecting flight, train and road options and journey times via Delhi
- Whether any metro or new public transport services have started
- Capitol Complex tour arrangements and ID requirements
- Rose Festival dates, and fog and AQI advisories in winter
