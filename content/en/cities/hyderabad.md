# Hyderabad city guide

Hyderabad, together with its twin city Secunderabad, is the capital of Telangana. The older city lies around the Charminar, and newer business districts such as HITEC City and Gachibowli lie to the west. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | Telangana |
| Main airport | Rajiv Gandhi International Airport (HYD), Shamshabad |
| Local language(s) | Telugu and Urdu; Hindi is widely understood. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |

| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Rajiv Gandhi International Airport is at Shamshabad, south of the city. An elevated expressway and the Outer Ring Road link it to the city. By road, allow roughly 45 minutes to over an hour to most central and western areas. Secunderabad and travel at peak times take longer.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official airport taxis or app-based cabs.** Book at the official counters or through an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Airport buses.** Pushpak buses, run by the state road transport corporation (TGSRTC), link the airport with several parts of the city. They suit you only if you are walking comfortably and carrying little luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** Check your flight's terminal arrangements, and confirm Pushpak bus routes and timings. A metro link to the airport has been planned; check whether it has opened.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | November–February | Pleasant and dry, with cool mornings and nights. These are the most comfortable months |
| Summer | March–May | Hot and dry. April and May can be very hot, with temperatures above 40°C |
| Monsoon | June–September | Moderate rain with occasional heavy downpours. Roads get waterlogged and traffic jams are common |
| Post-monsoon | October | Warm, with showers early in the month |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Heat.** In summer, stay indoors during the hottest hours, wear light, loose clothing and drink fluids as your care team advises.
- **Heavy downpours.** Sudden heavy rain can flood roads and slow traffic. Leave extra time for appointments.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.

## Getting around

- **Hyderabad Metro.** Three elevated lines (Red, Blue and Green) link many parts of the city. Stations involve stairs or escalators and can be busy at peak times.
- **App-based cabs and auto-rickshaws.** Cabs are the most practical option for most patients. For autos, book through an app or agree the fare first. Autos are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Buses.** TGSRTC city buses are crowded and not suitable while you are recovering.
- **Traffic.** Traffic is heavy at peak times, and the Old City's lanes are narrow and congested.
- **Walking.** Pavements are often uneven, the heat makes walking tiring, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Necklace Road and Hussain Sagar.** A flat promenade along the lake. Boats run to a large Buddha statue on an island. It is busy in the evenings.
- **KBR National Park, Jubilee Hills.** A walking track around the edge of a protected forest, popular in the early morning. The full loop is long, so walk only part of it.
- **Qutb Shahi Heritage Park.** The restored tombs of the Qutb Shahi dynasty in landscaped grounds. There is some uneven ground, and steps at the tombs.
- **Chowmahalla Palace.** A palace complex in the Old City, with courtyards and galleries. It is mostly flat but hot at midday.
- **Salar Jung Museum.** A large museum with an extensive art collection. The indoor galleries are long, so take breaks.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some outings until later in your recovery. Golconda Fort involves a steep climb on uneven stone. The Charminar and the bazaars around it have very crowded, narrow lanes. Avoid long day trips too.

## Food

Hyderabadi cuisine is known for dum biryani and kebabs. Other dishes include haleem (a slow-cooked meat and grain dish, especially popular during Ramadan), Irani chai with Osmania biscuits, and double ka meetha (a bread pudding). Telugu (Andhra-style) food is often very spicy. Halal food is widely available, as is vegetarian food. Some restaurants offer Jain meals.

Rich and spicy dishes may not suit you during recovery. Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Terminal arrangements at Rajiv Gandhi International Airport
- Pushpak airport bus routes and timings, and whether an airport metro link has opened
- Hyderabad Metro routes and extensions
- Opening days at museums, palaces and the heritage park
- Heavy rain advisories during the monsoon
