# Pune city guide

Pune is a large city inland from Mumbai, on the Deccan plateau. Because it is higher than the coast, its climate is milder and less humid than Mumbai's. Many international travellers reach Pune through Mumbai. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | Maharashtra |
| Main airport | Pune International Airport (PNQ), Lohegaon |
| Local language(s) | Marathi; Hindi is widely spoken. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |
| Time zone | India Standard Time (UTC+5:30) |
| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Pune International Airport is at Lohegaon, in the north-east of the city. It shares its runway with an Indian Air Force station and has only a limited number of international flights. Many international travellers fly into Mumbai and continue by domestic flight or by road. The drive from Mumbai, via the Mumbai–Pune Expressway, usually takes several hours, depending on traffic and where you start. From Pune airport, allow roughly 30 minutes to an hour by road to most parts of the city.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. If you arrive in Mumbai, ask your coordinator about a comfortable car for the longer transfer, with stops to stretch your legs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Public transport.** Links from Pune airport are limited, so a car is usually the simplest choice.

> **Check before you travel:** International routes into Pune change, so check your current options. If you connect via Mumbai, check which Mumbai airport you arrive at, because this affects the road time to Pune. Check which terminal your flight uses at Pune.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | November–February | Cool, dry and pleasant, with chilly nights, especially in December and January |
| Summer | March–May | Hot and dry, especially in April and May, but less humid than Mumbai |
| Monsoon | June–September | Steady rain, cooler and green. Heavy rain in the nearby hills (the Western Ghats) can disrupt roads, including the route to Mumbai |
| Post-monsoon | October | Warm, with occasional showers |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Heat.** In April and May, stay indoors during the hottest hours and drink fluids as your care team advises.
- **Monsoon travel.** If you are travelling between Pune and Mumbai during the monsoon, check road conditions first. Landslides and heavy traffic can slow the hill section.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.
- **Winter.** Pack a warm layer for cool evenings and early mornings.

## Getting around

- **Pune Metro.** Two lines (Purple and Aqua) are running, with more being built. Construction can cause road diversions.
- **App-based cabs and auto-rickshaws.** Cabs are the most practical option for most patients. Autos have meters, so ask for the meter to be used. Autos are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Buses.** PMPML city buses are crowded and not suitable while you are recovering.
- **Traffic.** Traffic is heavy at peak times, with many two-wheelers on the road.
- **Walking.** Pavements are often uneven or blocked, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Aga Khan Palace.** A palace set in gardens, where Mahatma Gandhi was held during the independence movement. It houses a memorial to Kasturba Gandhi, and the grounds are mostly flat.
- **Raja Dinkar Kelkar Museum.** A museum of everyday Indian objects and art in the old city. Its galleries are on several floors.
- **Pu La Deshpande Garden.** A Japanese-style garden with flat paths and water features.
- **Empress Botanical Garden.** An old botanical garden with large shade trees and mostly flat paths.
- **Shaniwar Wada.** The remains of an 18th-century palace-fort of the Peshwas. It is mostly flat inside, but the surfaces are uneven and there is little shade.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some trips until later in your recovery. These include hill forts such as Sinhagad, which involve steep climbs, monsoon trips into the Western Ghats such as Lonavala, with winding roads, crowds and a risk of landslides, and the crowds of the Ganesh festival (Ganeshotsav), usually around August or September.

## Food

Maharashtrian dishes include:

- misal pav (a spicy sprouted-bean curry with bread)
- poha
- sabudana khichdi
- thalipeeth (a multigrain flatbread)
- puran poli (a sweet flatbread)

Pune also has Irani cafés and food from across India and abroad. Vegetarian food is widely available, and many restaurants offer halal and Jain options.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Current international routes into Pune, or your connection via Mumbai and the road time from your Mumbai airport
- Which terminal your flight uses at Pune International Airport
- Pune Metro lines currently running
- Monsoon road advisories for the Western Ghats, including the Mumbai–Pune route
- Ganeshotsav dates, which change every year
