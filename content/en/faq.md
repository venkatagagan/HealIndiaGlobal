# Frequently asked questions

If your question is not answered here, message your coordinator in the app or open the Support screen.

> **Emergency:** If you have severe or sudden symptoms, do not use the app, chat or helpline to get help. Call your local emergency number now. In India, call 112.

## How Heal India Global works

### What is Heal India Global?

Heal India Global is a medical-travel facilitation platform. We help international patients find and compare Indian hospitals and doctors, request quotes, book and pay. We also organise visa support, travel, accommodation, translators and support while you are in India.

We are not a hospital, and we do not provide medical care. Your treatment is provided by the hospital and doctors you choose. They are responsible for your medical care and for all medical decisions.

### How does it work, step by step?

1. **Tell us about your condition.** Answer a short questionnaire, which can include optional AI screening, or search for a specialty, hospital or doctor yourself.
2. **See your matches.** We show hospitals and doctors that fit your needs and preferences.
3. **Compare and request quotes.** Send your case to the hospitals you choose and receive itemised quotes. Some doctors offer a video consultation.
4. **Book and pay.** Accept a quote, choose your dates and a payment option, and pay securely.
5. **Prepare to travel.** Your coordinator helps with the hospital letter for your visa, flights, airport pickup, accommodation and translators. The checklist in your Trips dashboard tracks progress.
6. **Treatment in India.** The hospital provides your care. We support you with logistics, messaging and a 24/7 helpline.
7. **Follow-up at home.** Keep your records in the app and arrange follow-up with your treating doctor and your local doctor.

### Do I have to use the AI screening?

No. AI screening is optional. You can switch off "Use AI" and we will match you with a simpler rules-based method. You can also skip the questionnaire and browse hospitals and doctors directly.

### Can I look around before creating an account?

Yes. You can browse as a guest. Some steps, such as uploading records, requesting quotes, booking and paying, need a full account. You can upgrade a guest account to a full account at any time.

### Can a family member or an agent help manage my case?

Yes. If a registered partner agent referred you or manages your case, they can start screenings, request quotes, book and pay on your behalf. You can add family members who travel with you as companions in your profile, so their details are ready for visa letters and bookings.

### How are hospitals and doctors matched to me?

Matching looks at:

- the specialty you need
- whether the hospital offers the treatment
- accreditation
- ratings
- the languages spoken
- your preferred doctor gender
- your budget
- availability during your travel window

The match percentage shows how well a hospital and doctor fit the information and preferences you gave us. It is **not** a measure of medical quality, and it does not predict the result of your treatment.

### How should I read ratings and success rates on profiles?

Ratings come from patient reviews, which are checked before they are published. Some profiles show a success rate. Those figures come from the hospital or doctor, and there is no single standard way of measuring them. Ask the doctor how the figure is calculated and what it means for someone in your situation. No figure on a profile predicts your own outcome.

### How do you check hospitals before listing them?

Every hospital goes through a review before it is listed, including checks of the registration and other documents it submits. Accreditations shown on a profile, such as JCI or NABH, are declared by the hospital and supported by documents it provides. You can confirm a hospital's current accreditation on the official website of the accrediting organisation. A listing is not a guarantee of results.

### How does Heal India Global make money?

Partner hospitals pay us a commission on bookings made through the platform. If a patient service fee ever applies to your booking, it appears as a separate line in your price summary before you pay.

## Costs and quotes

### How much will my treatment cost?

It depends on your condition and treatment plan, as well as:

- the hospital and doctor
- the room category
- any implants or medicines you need
- how long you stay

Any estimate shown before you have a quote is only a guide. Your price comes from the hospital's quote, after its doctors have reviewed your records.

### What does a quote include?

Quotes are itemised. The usual lines are:

- surgery or procedure
- hospital stay
- medicines
- tests and scans
- doctor fees
- implants
- other items

Each quote also shows the expected number of days in hospital and a "valid until" date. Read the inclusions and exclusions carefully, and ask your coordinator about anything that is unclear.

### Why could my final bill be different from the quote?

A quote is based on the records you shared. After examining you, the treating team may recommend a different plan, extra tests or a longer stay, and complications can happen. Changing to a different room category or adding services also changes the bill.

Whenever possible, ask the hospital to explain any change and give you a revised estimate before you agree to it.

### What is usually not included?

Unless your quote or package says otherwise, these are usually not included:

- flights and visa fees
- accommodation outside the hospital
- meals and accommodation for companions
- local transport and translators
- travel insurance
- medicines and follow-up care after you go home

Packages (Medical only, Medical + stay, All-inclusive) list exactly what they include.

### How long is a quote valid?

A quote is valid until the "valid until" date shown on it. After that date the hospital may need to update it, so ask your coordinator for a new quote.

### Can I compare several hospitals?

Yes. You can compare up to three matches side by side and request quotes from more than one hospital before you decide.

### Which currency will I pay in?

Hospitals usually price treatment in Indian rupees (INR). You can pay in any of the supported currencies shown at checkout. The amount is converted at the exchange rate fixed when your payment order is created, and you see the converted amount before you pay. Your bank or card issuer may add its own fees.

## Payments and refunds

### How can I pay?

- **Card or UPI**, through Razorpay's secure checkout. UPI usually needs a UPI app linked to a bank account that supports it.
- **International bank transfer (SWIFT).** The app shows the bank details and a unique payment reference. After paying, you upload your transfer receipt.
- **Payment plan.** Where it is offered, you can pay in instalments, for example over 3, 6 or 12 months.

The methods available to you depend on your booking and are shown at checkout.

### Do I have to pay everything up front?

No. You can pay in full, pay a deposit now and the balance later, or pay in instalments where available. Your booking is confirmed once you have paid at least the deposit. The deposit is 20% of the booking total by default. The deposit amount and due dates are shown before you pay.

### Is it safe to pay through the app?

Card payments are made on the payment provider's secure checkout, and card details are not stored on our servers. We will never ask for your card number, PIN, security code or one-time passwords by phone, email or chat. Pay only through the app or patient portal. Do not pay if a message asks you to send money to a different bank account; contact us through the app instead.

### What happens if I cancel?

Unless your hospital's or booking terms say otherwise, our default policy is:

| When you cancel | Refund of the amount paid towards your booking |
|---|---|
| 14 days or more before your admission date | 100% |
| 7 to 13 days before | 50% |
| Less than 7 days before | No refund |

The terms shown in your booking before you pay, including the hospital's own terms, are the ones that apply. See [Payments and refunds](payments-and-refunds.md) for details.

### How long does a refund take?

Approved refunds go back to the original payment method where possible. Bank transfers go back to the account they came from. How long a refund takes to appear depends on your bank and the payment provider; you can follow its status in the app. Exchange rates and bank charges can make the amount you receive in your currency slightly different from what you paid.

### Can I postpone instead of cancelling?

Talk to your coordinator before you cancel. The hospital may be able to move your dates, depending on availability. Whether a change of dates affects any refund depends on the hospital's and booking terms.

### Can I use my health insurance?

Some insurers cover treatment abroad, but many do not, and others need approval before treatment. Check with your insurer before you book. When booking, you can mark that you plan an insurance claim. We can then give you itemised receipts and help you get the medical documents your insurer asks for. Unless your insurer and the hospital agree otherwise in writing, you pay the hospital and claim the money back yourself.

## Visas

### What visa do I need?

Most international patients travel on India's **e-Medical visa**. Companions usually apply for an **e-Medical Attendant visa**, which is linked to the patient's visa. Eligibility depends on your nationality.

If you cannot get an e-Visa, you may be able to apply for a regular medical visa through the Indian embassy or consulate (or its authorised visa service) in your country.

Apply only through the official Government of India e-Visa website. Some unofficial websites look official and charge extra.

> **Check before you travel:** Visa types, eligibility, required documents, fees and processing times change. Check them on the official e-Visa website (https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/) before you apply.

### What do I need to apply?

The official website lists the current requirements. They usually include:

- a valid passport
- a recent photograph
- a scan of your passport's photo page
- a letter from the Indian hospital that will treat you

Your coordinator helps you get the hospital letter once your treatment is booked.

### Can you guarantee my visa?

No. Only the Government of India decides visa applications. We help with the hospital letter and your document checklist, and we track the status in your Trips dashboard. We cannot influence the decision or speed it up.

### What if my treatment takes longer than my visa allows?

Tell your coordinator as early as possible. Where an extension is possible, India's immigration authorities handle it through the online e-FRRO service of the Foreigners Regional Registration Office. You will usually need a letter from your hospital. Never overstay your visa.

### Do I need to register after I arrive?

That depends on your visa and how long you stay. Read the conditions on your visa and ask the hospital's international patient desk. Hotels and other accommodation in India must record the passport and visa details of foreign guests, so they will ask to see yours.

## Travel and accommodation

### Can you book my flights?

You can request flights, airport pickup and drop-off, and local transport in the app. Your coordinator confirms the details and price before anything is booked. Choose flexible or changeable tickets where you can, because discharge dates and the date you are fit to fly can change.

### Will someone meet me at the airport?

If you booked airport pickup, your coordinator shares the driver's details before you land. The driver usually waits in the arrivals area with a sign showing your name. Only get into the car arranged for you, and ignore offers from people who approach you in the terminal.

### How soon after treatment can I fly home?

Only when your treating doctor says you are fit to fly. This depends on your procedure and how you recover. Some airlines ask for a medical clearance form (often called a MEDIF) after recent surgery. If you need a wheelchair or other help at the airport, book it with the airline in advance. See the procedure guides and [post-treatment care](post-treatment-care.md).

### Where will I stay?

The options include:

- hospital guest rooms
- service apartments with kitchens
- guest houses
- hotels

Most are close to your hospital. When you choose, think about:

- the distance to the hospital
- lifts and step-free access
- a kitchen if you follow a special diet
- room for your companion

You can request accommodation in the app.

### Can my companion stay with me in hospital?

Many hospitals let one attendant stay in the patient's room, depending on the room type and hospital rules. Intensive care units have strict visiting rules. Ask the international patient desk before you travel.

## Language support and translators

### Will the doctors speak my language?

Doctors in larger Indian hospitals usually speak English. Many hospitals have international patient desks that can arrange interpreters for some languages. Hospital and doctor profiles list the languages spoken, and you can filter your search by language.

### How do I get a translator?

Request one in the app (Support, then Get Translator) or ask your coordinator. Interpreters may come from the hospital or from an independent provider. Any charge is shown before the booking is confirmed.

For consent forms and major decisions, ask for a qualified medical interpreter. Do not rely only on a family member or a translation app.

## Safety

### Is it safe to travel to India for treatment?

Any medical trip needs careful planning. To reduce the risks:

- Choose an accredited hospital and share your full medical history with it.
- Buy suitable travel insurance.
- Check your government's travel advice for India.
- Follow food and water precautions.

The [in-country guide](in-country-guide.md) explains how to stay safe and comfortable in India.

### What happens if something goes wrong during my treatment?

The hospital's medical team is responsible for your clinical care, including managing any complications. Your coordinator can help with communication and logistics, such as extending your accommodation or asking the hospital for a letter if you need more time on your visa.

If you have concerns about your care, raise them with the international patient desk and tell your coordinator. We will help you reach the right people. Keep copies of all your records.

### What should I do in an emergency in India?

Call **112**, India's national emergency number. Ambulance services on **108** operate in many states; confirm the local number when you arrive. Then tell your hospital and press SOS in the app so your coordinator and our support team are alerted. The SOS button does not call an ambulance.

## Your data and privacy

### Who can see my medical information?

Only people who need it to help you:

- the hospitals you choose to share your case with, after you consent
- our coordinators and support team
- your agent, if one manages your case

Hospitals you have not chosen do not see your medical information. See [privacy in plain language](privacy-in-plain-language.md).

### How is my data protected?

- Your connection to our servers is encrypted.
- Sensitive details, such as passport numbers, insurance policy numbers and bank details, are encrypted in our database and shown only partly, for example the last four digits.
- People see only what their role and your case allow.
- Document downloads are permission-checked.
- Important actions are logged.

You can also turn on two-step verification for your account.

### Can I change my mind about consent?

Yes. You can review and change your consents in your profile at any time. Withdrawing a consent stops future use of your information for that purpose. It does not undo sharing that has already happened, such as records a hospital already holds for your care. Marketing messages are optional.

## AI screening: limits and emergencies

### Is the AI screening a diagnosis?

No. It is an information tool. It suggests which specialties and treatments may be worth discussing with a doctor, and which questions to ask. It can be wrong or incomplete. Only a qualified doctor who examines you and reviews your records can diagnose your condition or recommend treatment.

### What happens to the information I give the AI?

If you consent, an AI service processes your answers, and up to three reports you choose to attach, to produce a summary. If you do not consent, or you switch off "Use AI", we use a simpler rules-based method instead. Hospitals see your screening only if you choose to send them your case.

### What if the screening says my symptoms are urgent?

If the screening finds warning signs, such as chest pain at rest, signs of a stroke, severe bleeding or difficulty breathing, it tells you to get emergency care where you are **now** instead of planning travel. Please do that. Do not wait for a match, a quote or a reply from us.

### Can I use the app in a medical emergency?

No. The app, the chat and our helpline are not emergency services. Call your local emergency number. In India, call 112.

## Companions and attendants

### Should I bring someone with me?

For major surgery or a long stay, we strongly recommend travelling with a companion. Some treatments, such as transplants, need a dedicated carer, and organ transplants also need a donor. A companion can support you, help with communication and help you travel home.

### What visa does my companion need?

A companion travelling with a patient usually applies for the e-Medical Attendant visa, linked to the patient's e-Medical visa. The Government of India sets how many attendants are allowed and who can apply; check the official e-Visa website. Organ donors may need a different visa or extra documents, so ask your coordinator.

### Does my companion need travel insurance?

We recommend that everyone who travels has their own travel insurance. Add companions to your profile so their details are ready for visa letters and bookings.

## After you return home

### What will I receive when I leave hospital?

Before you leave, you should receive:

- a discharge summary
- your medicine list
- instructions for wound or other care
- the warning signs to watch for
- your follow-up plan

Keep these in the app's Documents section. See [post-treatment care](post-treatment-care.md).

### Can I talk to my Indian doctor after I go home?

Many doctors offer video or phone follow-up consultations. Where one is available, you can request it in the app. A fee may apply, and it is shown before you confirm. Your local doctor should also be part of your follow-up.

### What if I have a problem after I get home?

If you have severe or sudden symptoms, call your local emergency number. For problems that are not urgent, see your local doctor and show them your discharge summary. You can also message your treating hospital through the app.

### Can my local doctor speak to my doctor in India?

Often, yes. Ask your coordinator to help arrange it. You can share your records with your local doctor from the Documents section.
