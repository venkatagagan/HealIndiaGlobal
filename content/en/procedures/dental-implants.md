# Dental implants

This page explains dental implant treatment and why it often takes more than one trip to India. It is general information to help you plan and to prepare questions for your dentist.

## What it is

A dental implant is a small post, usually made of titanium and sometimes of zirconia. It is placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Over time the bone bonds with the implant, a process called osseointegration. A crown, bridge or denture is then fixed on top.

Implants can replace a single tooth, several teeth or a full arch. Placement is a minor surgical procedure, usually done under local anaesthesia, sometimes with sedation.

If there is not enough bone to hold an implant, you may need extra procedures first:

- a **bone graft**, which builds up the jawbone
- a **sinus lift**, which adds bone below the sinus in the upper jaw

These can lengthen the overall timeline.

## Why doctors may recommend it

Dentists may suggest implants to replace missing teeth, or to hold a denture firmly in place, when the jawbone and gums are healthy enough. Some things can affect healing and may need attention first, or may make implants unsuitable:

- gum disease
- smoking
- poorly controlled diabetes
- some medicines that affect bone, including certain osteoporosis treatments
- previous radiotherapy to the head and neck

Alternatives include bridges, removable dentures or leaving the gap. Only your dentist can advise, after an examination and scans. Ask what result is realistic for your mouth, including how your teeth will look and work.

## Records to share for a quote

- Recent dental X-rays: a panoramic X-ray (OPG) and, if you have one, a 3D cone-beam CT (CBCT) scan, ideally the original image files rather than photos of them
- Clear, well-lit photos of your teeth and smile from the front and both sides
- Your dentist's notes or treatment plan, and details of previous treatment such as extractions, root canals or grafts
- Your medical history, including diabetes, heart conditions, bleeding problems, osteoporosis treatment and any cancer treatment
- Your current medicines (especially blood thinners and bone medicines), any allergies, and whether you smoke

## Tests usually done before treatment

- A dental examination and a check of your gums
- A panoramic X-ray (OPG) and a CBCT scan to measure the bone and plan where each implant goes
- Impressions or a digital scan of your teeth
- Blood tests if needed, for example blood sugar if you have diabetes, or clotting tests if you take blood thinners
- Treatment of gum disease or decay first, if needed

Your hospital will tell you which tests you need; some may be repeated in India.

## Typical time in hospital and in India

| Stage | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital stay | None (day procedures) | Treatment takes place in a dental clinic |
| First visit: implant placement | About 5–7 days in India | Assessment, scans, surgery and a check |
| Healing at home | Usually a few months | The bone bonds with the implant; your dentist decides how long |
| Second visit: final teeth | About 5–10 days in India | Fitting and adjusting the permanent crown, bridge or denture |

These are typical ranges only. Your time varies by patient; your doctor will advise.

Some clinics offer temporary teeth fitted at the same visit as the implants (sometimes called immediate loading) for suitable patients. Bone grafts and sinus lifts usually add healing time.

## Recovery

### In India

- Swelling, bruising and discomfort for a few days are common. Use cold packs, eat soft foods and take pain relief as advised.
- Avoid chewing on the implant area. Clean your mouth gently exactly as instructed, for example with a soft brush and a prescribed mouthwash or salt-water rinses.
- Take any antibiotics or other medicines exactly as prescribed.
- Avoid smoking, which slows healing and increases the risk of problems.
- Ask whether your stitches dissolve or need removing before you leave.

### After you return home

- During healing, avoid hard or sticky food on the implant area. If you have temporary teeth, care for them as advised.
- See your local dentist for checks while you wait for the second visit.
- Once your final teeth are fitted, implants need careful daily cleaning and regular dental check-ups. Infection of the gum and bone around an implant (peri-implantitis) can develop if it is not kept clean.
- Keep a written record of your implants (make, system and size), so a dentist at home can maintain or repair them.

## Fitness to fly

- **Sinus lift:** if you had a sinus lift, ask your surgeon specifically about flying, because changes in cabin pressure can affect a healing sinus. Also ask how to blow your nose and sneeze safely.
- **Bleeding and swelling:** carry any gauze and medicines you were given in your hand luggage.
- **Blood clots:** long flights carry a general risk of blood clots (deep vein thrombosis, DVT). Walk and stretch regularly.
- **Sedation:** if you had sedation, follow the clinic's advice about when you can travel.
- **Paperwork:** medical clearance from the airline (often called a MEDIF) is not usually needed after routine dental treatment. Ask your dentist if you are unsure.

Your treating doctor confirms when you are fit to fly, usually at a review before discharge or shortly after.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Am I suitable for implants, and what are the alternatives for me?
- How many implants do I need, and which implant system will you use? Will I get the details in writing?
- Do I need a bone graft or a sinus lift?
- What is the full treatment timeline, and how many visits to India will I need?
- Can I have temporary teeth while the implants heal?
- What does the quote include, for example scans, temporary teeth, final crowns and follow-up visits?
- What happens if an implant does not bond with the bone?
- When can I fly, especially if I have a sinus lift?
- How should I clean and care for my implants?
- Can a dentist in my country maintain and repair this work?

## When to get urgent help

> **Emergency:** Get urgent help if you have:
>
> - bleeding that does not stop with firm pressure
> - swelling that spreads, especially towards the eye, neck or floor of the mouth
> - difficulty swallowing or breathing
> - a fever, or you feel very unwell
> - severe pain that gets worse after the first few days
> - numbness of the lip, chin or tongue that does not wear off after the anaesthetic (contact your dentist promptly)
>
> In India, call 112 or the hospital. At home, call your local emergency number.
