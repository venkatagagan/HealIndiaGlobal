# In vitro fertilisation (IVF)

This page gives a general explanation of IVF. It can help you plan your trip and prepare questions for your fertility specialist. Your own doctors will advise on your situation.

> **Important:** India regulates assisted reproduction and surrogacy by law. The two main laws are the Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 and the Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021. They set rules on who is eligible for treatment, on the use of donor eggs, sperm and embryos, and on surrogacy. These rules can change. Commercial surrogacy is not permitted. Before you plan treatment or travel, check the current rules that apply to you with the clinic and with official Government of India sources.

## What it is

In IVF, eggs are collected from the ovaries and fertilised with sperm in a laboratory. The embryos that result grow in the laboratory for a few days. Then one embryo, or sometimes more, is placed in the uterus (womb). A typical cycle has these steps:

1. **Ovarian stimulation:** you have daily hormone injections, usually for one to two weeks, to help several eggs mature. Ultrasound scans and blood tests check your progress.
2. **Egg collection:** a short procedure under sedation or anaesthesia. A fine needle, guided by ultrasound, collects eggs from the ovaries. It is usually a day procedure.
3. **Fertilisation:** the eggs are mixed with sperm. Alternatively, a single sperm is injected into each egg. This is called ICSI.
4. **Embryo transfer:** a thin tube places an embryo in the uterus. This usually does not need anaesthesia. A fresh transfer happens a few days after egg collection. A frozen transfer uses a frozen embryo in a later cycle.
5. **Pregnancy test:** usually about two weeks after the transfer. Your clinic will tell you when.

Embryos that are not transferred may be frozen. The law and the clinic's own policies set the rules on how they are stored, used and disposed of.

## Why doctors may recommend it

Doctors may suggest IVF in situations such as:

- blocked or damaged fallopian tubes
- a low sperm count or poor sperm quality
- problems with ovulation
- endometriosis
- infertility with no clear cause
- other fertility treatments have not worked

Only your doctors can decide whether IVF is right for you. Ask about the alternatives, such as medicines, intrauterine insemination (IUI) or treatment of a specific cause.

## Records to share for a quote

Each partner (where applicable) should upload:

- Fertility test results, such as hormone blood tests, pelvic ultrasound reports and semen analysis
- Records of previous fertility treatments, including the medicines used, how you responded and any embryo reports
- Reports of operations or investigations, such as laparoscopy, hysteroscopy or tests of the fallopian tubes
- Infection screening results and blood groups
- Medical history, a list of medicines and any allergies

The clinic will also tell you which identity documents and other papers it needs under Indian law.

## Tests usually done before treatment

**Female partner:** typical tests include:

- hormone blood tests
- a pelvic ultrasound to check the ovaries and uterus
- thyroid and other blood tests
- infection screening, such as for HIV, hepatitis B and C, and syphilis
- blood group

Some women also need an examination of the inside of the uterus, such as a hysteroscopy.

**Male partner:** semen analysis, infection screening and blood group.

Some tests can be done at home before you travel. Your hospital will tell you which tests you need. Some may be repeated in India.

## Typical time in hospital and in India

| Stage | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital stay | Usually none | Egg collection is usually a day procedure, and you go home the same day |
| Total time in India | About 2–4 weeks per cycle | Depends on the treatment plan; some plans start at home, and a frozen transfer may need a later trip |

These are typical ranges only. Your time varies by patient; your doctor will advise.

## Recovery

### In India

Mild cramps, bloating and light spotting are common after egg collection. Most people rest for the rest of that day. After the embryo transfer, you can usually return to light everyday activities. Your clinic will tell you:

- which medicines to take, often hormone support
- which activities to avoid
- when to do the pregnancy test

Your clinic will also watch for ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS). This is a reaction to the fertility medicines. It can make the ovaries swell and cause fluid to build up in the body. Learn the warning signs listed below.

### After you return home

- Keep taking any prescribed medicines until the clinic tells you to stop.
- Do the pregnancy test when the clinic advises, and share the result with them.
- If you are pregnant, arrange early pregnancy care with a doctor at home and share your treatment records.
- If the cycle is not successful, ask for a follow-up consultation to review it and talk about your options.
- IVF can be emotionally demanding. Counselling and support groups can help, and many clinics can suggest where to find them.

## Fitness to fly

Your treating doctor will confirm when you are fit to fly. This is usually at a review before you leave or shortly after. Things to discuss:

- **After egg collection:** do not fly if you have signs of OHSS. Ask your doctor to check you first.
- **After embryo transfer:** ask the clinic when you can travel. Also ask what to do if you have pain or bleeding while you are away.
- **Blood clots (DVT):** fertility hormones and pregnancy can increase the risk of clots. Ask whether you need compression stockings. Choose an aisle seat, drink water and move regularly.
- **Medicines:** some fertility medicines need to be kept cold, and some come with needles. Carry them in your hand luggage with your prescription and a letter from the clinic. Check your airline's rules for needles and cool packs.
- **Airline rules:** ask your clinic whether you need a letter for the airline.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Which treatment plan do you recommend for us, and why?
- How many days do we need to be in India, and when should we arrive?
- Do you suggest a fresh or a frozen embryo transfer?
- How many embryos would you transfer? What are the risks of transferring more than one, such as twins?
- What happens to unused embryos, and what does the law allow?
- What does the law allow in our situation, for example on donor eggs or sperm?
- What is my risk of OHSS, and how will you reduce it?
- Which medicines will we take home, and how should we store them?
- Which costs does the quote include, for example medicines, embryo freezing and storage?

## When to get urgent help

> **Emergency:** Get help immediately if you have any of these:
>
> - severe pain or swelling in the abdomen
> - rapid weight gain
> - shortness of breath
> - passing very little urine
> - vomiting that does not stop
> - heavy bleeding from the vagina
> - a fever
> - fainting
> - sharp pain on one side of the abdomen
> - a painful, swollen leg
> - chest pain
>
> In India, call 112 or the hospital. At home, call your local emergency number.
