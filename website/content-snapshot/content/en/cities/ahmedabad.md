# Ahmedabad city guide

Ahmedabad is the largest city in Gujarat and lies on the Sabarmati river. The state capital, Gandhinagar, is a short drive to the north. The old walled city, east of the river, is a UNESCO World Heritage City, and newer residential and business areas spread to the west. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

|---|---|
| State/territory | Gujarat |

## Arriving by air

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport is on the northern side of the city. By road, allow roughly 20 minutes to an hour, depending on where you are going and the traffic. Gandhinagar is also within reach by road.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Public transport.** Links to the airport are limited, so a car is usually the simplest choice.

> **Check before you travel:** International flights have used Terminal 2, but allocations change, so check your ticket. Ask your coordinator whether any new public transport links to the airport have opened.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | November–February | Pleasant, dry days, with cool nights, especially in December and January |
| Summer | March–June | Very hot and dry. Temperatures can exceed 40°C, especially in May, and heat alerts are common |
| Monsoon | July–September | Moderate rain with occasional heavy downpours. It is humid, and waterlogging is possible |
| Post-monsoon | October | Hot at first, then drier and more comfortable |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Heat.** Follow local heat alerts in summer. Stay indoors during the hottest hours and drink fluids as your care team advises.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.
- **Festivals.** Navratri, usually around September or October, brings nine nights of late crowds and music. Uttarayan, the kite festival in mid-January, fills rooftops and streets. Kite strings can cause injuries, especially to people on two-wheelers, so take care outdoors.

## Getting around

- **Ahmedabad Metro.** Two lines cross the city, and an extension runs towards Gandhinagar.
- **Buses.** BRTS (Janmarg) buses run in dedicated lanes, alongside AMTS city buses. Both are crowded at peak times and not suitable while you are recovering.
- **App-based cabs and auto-rickshaws.** Cabs are the most practical option for most patients. For autos, ask for the meter or agree the fare first. Autos are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Traffic.** Roads are busy at peak times, especially in and around the old city.
- **Walking.** Pavements are often uneven, the heat makes walking tiring, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

> **Check before you travel:** Gujarat has alcohol prohibition laws, with a permit system for some visitors. Alcohol is generally not advisable during treatment and recovery. If it matters to you, check the current rules.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Sabarmati Riverfront.** Long, flat promenades with gardens along both banks of the river. Go in the early morning or evening.
- **Sabarmati Ashram (Gandhi Ashram).** Mahatma Gandhi's former home by the river, with a museum. It is mostly flat. Redevelopment works have been under way, so check what is open.
- **Kankaria Lake.** A lake with a walking path around it, busy in the evenings.
- **Calico Museum of Textiles.** A textile museum that you visit on a guided tour booked in advance.
- **Hutheesing Jain Temple.** A 19th-century carved marble temple. It is mostly flat. Remove your shoes and dress modestly.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some outings until later in your recovery. These include stepwells such as Adalaj, which have many steps, walks through the old city's narrow and uneven lanes, crowded Navratri events and rooftop gatherings during Uttarayan.

## Food

Gujarati food is largely vegetarian. A thali usually includes dal, kadhi (a yoghurt curry), rotli (flatbread), rice, vegetables and snacks (farsan) such as dhokla and khandvi. Many dishes taste slightly sweet, and undhiyu, a mixed vegetable dish, is a winter speciality. Jain meals, made without onion, garlic or root vegetables, are widely available. Non-vegetarian and halal food can be found in some areas and restaurants.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Which terminal your flight uses at Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport
- Public transport links to the airport, if any
- Ahmedabad Metro lines and the status of the Gandhinagar extension
- Current alcohol prohibition and permit rules, if relevant
- Visiting arrangements at Sabarmati Ashram during redevelopment, Calico Museum tour bookings and Kankaria Lake entry rules
- Festival dates: Navratri changes every year; Uttarayan is in mid-January
