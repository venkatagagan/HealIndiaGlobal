# Bengaluru city guide

Bengaluru (also called Bangalore) is the capital of Karnataka. It sits on a plateau about 900 metres above sea level, so its climate is milder than in most large Indian cities. It is also known for heavy traffic, so plan your journeys carefully. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## Arriving by air

Kempegowda International Airport is well north of the city. The road transfer to central Bengaluru often takes about an hour. To the south or east of the city it can take one to two hours or more, depending on traffic.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs, especially as the transfer is long. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official airport taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Book at the counters or through an app, and board at the marked pick-up zones.
3. **Airport buses.** BMTC runs air-conditioned airport buses (Vayu Vajra) to many parts of the city. They suit you only if you are walking comfortably and carrying little luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** The airport has two terminals, so check which one your airline uses. A metro line to the airport has been under construction; check whether it has opened. Confirm airport bus routes and timings before you rely on them.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | December–February | Pleasant, dry days, with cool mornings and nights |
| Summer | March–May | Warm to hot afternoons, usually less extreme than in much of India. Thunderstorms are possible in May |
| Southwest monsoon | June–September | Frequent moderate showers, cloudier and cooler |
| Post-monsoon | October–November | Showers continue and can be heavy, especially in October, then it gradually dries out |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Mild, but not cold.** Pack a light layer for cool evenings and air-conditioned rooms. Even in mild weather, drink fluids as your care team advises.
- **Rain.** Heavy downpours can flood underpasses and roads quickly and slow traffic even more. Leave extra time for appointments.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the rains. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.

## Getting around

- **Namma Metro.** The Purple, Green and Yellow lines are running, and more lines are being built. Construction can cause road diversions.
- **App-based cabs and auto-rickshaws.** Cabs are the most practical option for most patients. Autos are often booked through apps; otherwise, agree the fare before you set off. Autos are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Buses.** BMTC city buses are crowded at peak times and not suitable while you are recovering.
- **Traffic.** Traffic is heavy and journey times are hard to predict. Leave plenty of time for appointments.
- **Walking.** Pavements are often broken or uneven, there is a lot of construction, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Cubbon Park.** A large central park with shaded paths. It is quieter on weekday mornings.
- **Lalbagh Botanical Garden.** A botanical garden with a glasshouse and a lake. Distances inside are long, so choose a short loop. The flower shows, usually held around Republic Day (January) and Independence Day (August), draw very large crowds.
- **Visvesvaraya Industrial and Technological Museum.** An indoor science and technology museum next to Cubbon Park, spread over several floors.
- **National Gallery of Modern Art.** An art gallery in a heritage mansion with gardens. It is usually quiet.
- **Bangalore Palace.** A 19th-century Tudor-style palace with grounds. Expect some walking and stairs.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave long day trips until later in your recovery. These include Mysuru, several hours away by road, and Nandi Hills, which is reached by a winding hill road.

## Food

Karnataka food includes masala dosa, idli, vada, bisi bele bath (spiced rice and lentils) and ragi mudde (finger-millet balls). Vegetarian "Udupi" restaurants and quick-service "darshini" eateries are common. Food from across India and international food are widely available. Vegetarian food is everywhere. Halal food is widely available, and many vegetarian restaurants offer Jain meals.

Bengaluru has many pubs and breweries. Alcohol is generally not advisable during recovery and can interact with medicines, so ask your doctor. Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Which terminal your airline uses at Kempegowda International Airport
- Airport bus routes and timings, and whether the airport metro line has opened
- Namma Metro lines currently running
- Heavy rain and traffic advisories
- Lalbagh flower show dates, and opening days and rules at parks, museums and the palace
