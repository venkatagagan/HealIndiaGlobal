# Chennai city guide

Chennai is a large coastal city on the Bay of Bengal and the capital of Tamil Nadu. Hospitals are spread across the city, including along the southern IT corridor on Old Mahabalipuram Road (OMR). This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | Tamil Nadu |
| Main airport | Chennai International Airport (MAA), Meenambakkam |
| Local language(s) | Tamil. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |
| Time zone | India Standard Time (UTC+5:30) |
| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Chennai International Airport is in the south-west of the city. By road, allow roughly 30 minutes to an hour to central Chennai. Journeys take longer at peak times, and to areas further south along OMR.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Chennai Metro.** The Blue Line has a station at the airport, linked to the terminals by walkways. The metro suits you only if you are walking comfortably and carrying little luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** The airport's terminals have been rebuilt and reorganised in recent years, so check which terminal your flight uses. Confirm the metro connection and its operating hours before you rely on it.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | January–February | Warm days, slightly cooler nights and lower humidity. These are the most comfortable months |
| Summer | March–June | Hot and humid, hottest around May and June |
| Southwest monsoon | July–September | Hot and humid, with occasional thunderstorms. Chennai gets less rain in these months than India's west coast |
| Northeast monsoon | October–December | Chennai's main rainy season. Heavy rain floods roads, and cyclones can occur, particularly in November and December |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Heat.** Chennai is hot for most of the year. Stay indoors during the hottest hours, wear light, loose clothing and drink fluids as your care team advises.
- **Rain and cyclones.** If you travel between October and December, follow the weather warnings from the India Meteorological Department (IMD). Keep your plans flexible in case flights or roads are disrupted.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the rains. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.
- **The sea.** Currents along Chennai's beaches are strong and swimming is dangerous.

## Getting around

- **Chennai Metro.** The Blue and Green lines are running, and a large second phase is being built. The construction can cause diversions and congestion.
- **App-based cabs.** These are the most practical option for most patients.
- **Auto-rickshaws.** Drivers often do not use meters, so book through an app or agree the fare before you set off. Autos are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Buses and suburban trains.** They are crowded and not suitable while you are recovering.
- **Traffic.** Traffic is heavy at peak times, and roads can flood quickly in heavy rain.
- **Walking.** Pavements are often uneven, the heat makes walking tiring, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Marina Beach promenade.** A long seafront with a paved walkway. It is pleasant in the early morning or evening. Do not swim.
- **Elliot's Beach, Besant Nagar.** A smaller, generally quieter beach with cafés nearby.
- **Theosophical Society gardens, Adyar.** Large, quiet grounds with old trees. Visiting hours are limited.
- **Government Museum, Egmore.** A museum with galleries in several buildings across one campus, so expect some walking between them.
- **Kapaleeshwarar Temple, Mylapore.** A Dravidian-style temple with a tall gateway tower (gopuram). Remove your shoes and dress modestly, and note that stone floors can get very hot underfoot. Some areas may be open only to Hindu worshippers, so follow the signs. It is crowded at festival times.
- **San Thome Basilica, Mylapore.** A Catholic basilica near the beach. It is usually calm. Dress modestly.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some trips until later in your recovery. The DakshinaChitra heritage centre and the monuments at Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram) are longer drives south along East Coast Road and involve a lot of walking on uneven ground. Festivals such as Pongal in January and Diwali bring crowds and heavy traffic.

## Food

Tamil food includes idli, dosa, vada, sambar, rasam and filter coffee. Many restaurants serve "meals": a vegetarian set meal of rice with several dishes, often on a banana leaf. Chettinad cooking, from southern Tamil Nadu, is known for spicy, aromatic meat dishes, and seafood is common. Vegetarian food is everywhere. Halal food is available in many areas. Some vegetarian restaurants offer Jain meals if you ask in advance.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Which terminal your flight uses, and the metro connection at the airport
- New metro sections opening and any construction diversions
- IMD warnings for heavy rain and cyclones, especially from October to December
- Visiting hours at the Theosophical Society and the museum
- Dress codes and access rules at temples and other places of worship
