# Delhi NCR city guide

The National Capital Region (NCR) is a large urban area. Hospitals are spread across Delhi, Gurugram, Noida, Faridabad and Ghaziabad, so travel times depend on where you are treated and where you stay. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | National Capital Territory of Delhi, plus parts of Haryana (Gurugram, Faridabad) and Uttar Pradesh (Noida, Ghaziabad) |
| Main airport | Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL), New Delhi |
| Local language(s) | Hindi; Punjabi and Urdu are also spoken. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |
| Time zone | India Standard Time (UTC+5:30) |
| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Indira Gandhi International Airport is in south-west Delhi. By road, allow roughly 30 minutes to over an hour to central Delhi or Gurugram. Noida, Faridabad and Ghaziabad often take longer, depending on traffic.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Book only at counters inside the terminal or through an app, and ignore offers from people who approach you in the arrivals hall.
3. **Delhi Metro.** The Airport Express Line runs from Terminal 3, and Terminal 1 has a Magenta Line station. The metro suits you only if you are walking comfortably with little luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** International flights mainly use Terminal 3, but terminal allocations change, so check your ticket. A second airport for the region, Noida International Airport at Jewar, has been under development. Check which airport your flight uses, because the two are far apart.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | December–January | Cool days and cold nights. Dense fog, especially in the mornings, can delay flights and slow road travel |
| Spring | February–March | Mild, pleasant days that warm up quickly |
| Summer | April–June | Very hot and dry. Temperatures can exceed 40°C, with hot winds and dust |
| Monsoon | July–September | Humid with heavy showers. Roads get waterlogged and traffic jams are common |
| Post-monsoon | October–November | Drier and cooler, but air pollution often becomes severe |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Air quality.** Air pollution is often very poor from around October or November to January. Check the daily Air Quality Index (AQI) published by India's Central Pollution Control Board. If you have a heart or lung condition, or have had chest or heart surgery, ask your doctor about wearing a mask (such as an N95) and limiting time outdoors.
- **Heat.** In summer, stay indoors during the hottest hours. Drink fluids as your care team advises; some heart and kidney patients have fluid limits.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.
- **Fog.** If you fly home in December or January, leave a buffer in your plans for delays.

## Getting around

- **Delhi Metro.** A large, air-conditioned network links Delhi with Gurugram, Noida, Faridabad and Ghaziabad. Stations can involve long walks, stairs and security queues, and trains are crowded at peak hours. The first coach in the direction of travel is reserved for women.
- **App-based cabs.** These are widely available. Check that the number plate matches the app before you get in.
- **Auto-rickshaws.** They are open-sided, bumpy and exposed to heat and pollution, so they are not ideal after surgery. Agree the fare or ask for the meter.
- **Traffic.** Traffic is heavy, and journeys between parts of the NCR can take a long time. Leave plenty of time for appointments.
- **Walking.** Pavements are often uneven or blocked, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

> **Check before you travel:** On high-pollution days, Delhi NCR's Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) can restrict some vehicles and activities. Check which restrictions currently apply.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Lodhi Garden.** A large public park with mostly flat paths around historic tombs. It is busy with walkers in the early morning and evening.
- **Sunder Nursery.** A landscaped heritage park next to Humayun's Tomb, with paved paths and shade.
- **Humayun's Tomb.** A UNESCO World Heritage Site. The gardens are mostly flat, but steep steps lead up to the tomb itself and the stone surfaces are uneven.
- **Dilli Haat (INA).** An open-air crafts and food market. It is mostly flat but crowded in the evenings and at weekends.
- **Air-conditioned shopping malls.** Malls in Delhi, Gurugram and Noida have lifts and seating, which makes them useful for a short, cool walk on hot or high-pollution days.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some outings until later in your recovery. These include the busy lanes of Old Delhi and Chandni Chowk, large monuments such as the Red Fort, and day trips such as Agra, which mean several hours on the road.

## Food

Delhi's food reflects North Indian traditions: Punjabi dishes such as dal makhani and parathas, Mughlai kebabs and biryanis, and street snacks such as chaat. Vegetarian food is everywhere and halal food is widely available. Many restaurants can prepare Jain meals if you ask in advance.

Much restaurant food is rich, oily or spicy. Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Which airport (Indira Gandhi International or Noida International) and which terminal your flight uses
- Metro stations at the airport (Airport Express Line and Magenta Line) and their operating hours
- Delhi Metro routes, extensions and coach rules
- The daily AQI and any GRAP restrictions in winter
- Fog-related flight disruption in December and January
- Opening days, entry rules and visitor arrangements at parks, monuments and markets
