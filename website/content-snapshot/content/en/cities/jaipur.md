# Jaipur city guide

Jaipur is the capital of Rajasthan. It has a historic walled centre, the "Pink City", which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and newer areas that spread south and west. Jaipur is a major tourist destination, so its sights get crowded, especially between October and March. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## Arriving by air

Jaipur International Airport is at Sanganer, south of the city. By road, allow roughly 20 to 45 minutes to central Jaipur, depending on traffic. Some international travellers fly into Delhi and continue by domestic flight, by train or by road. The drive from Delhi takes several hours.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Public transport.** Links to the airport are limited, so a car is usually the simplest choice.

> **Check before you travel:** Check which terminal your flight uses and which international routes currently serve Jaipur. If you connect via Delhi, check flight, train and road options and journey times.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | December–January | Sunny, pleasant days and cold nights |
| Spring | February–March | Warm, pleasant days that heat up through March |
| Summer | April–June | Very hot and dry. Temperatures often exceed 40°C, with hot winds and dust |
| Monsoon | July–September | Humid, with heavy showers on and off and occasional waterlogging |
| Post-monsoon | October–November | Warm days and cooling nights. The tourist season begins |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Heat.** In summer, avoid going out in the middle of the day and drink fluids as your care team advises.
- **Dust.** Dry, dusty conditions can irritate the airways. If you have a lung condition, ask your doctor whether you should wear a mask outdoors.
- **Cold nights.** Pack warm layers for winter evenings, early mornings and air-conditioned rooms.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.

## Getting around

- **Jaipur Metro.** One line runs from Mansarovar to Badi Chaupar in the walled city, so it covers only a limited area.
- **App-based cabs.** These are the most practical option for most patients.
- **Auto-rickshaws and e-rickshaws.** Agree the fare before you set off. They are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Buses.** City buses are crowded and not suitable while you are recovering.
- **Traffic.** Roads are congested, especially in the walled city, and you may meet animals such as cows on the road.
- **Walking.** Lanes in the old city are narrow and uneven, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Albert Hall Museum.** Rajasthan's state museum, set in Ram Niwas Garden, with indoor galleries and flat gardens around it.
- **Jantar Mantar.** An early 18th-century astronomical observatory and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is open-air and mostly flat but has little shade, so go early.
- **City Palace.** A palace complex with courtyards and museum galleries. Some parts involve stairs.
- **Central Park.** A large park with a long walking track. It suits short walks in the early morning.
- **Hawa Mahal.** You can view its façade from the street. Going inside involves ramps and climbing, so save that for later.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some outings until later in your recovery. Amber (Amer) Fort, Nahargarh and Jaigarh involve steep climbs and uneven paths. Also avoid crowded festival events and long drives to other cities.

## Food

Rajasthani dishes include:

- dal baati churma (lentils with baked wheat balls and a sweet crumble)
- gatte ki sabzi (gram-flour dumplings in a yoghurt curry)
- ker sangri (a dish of desert beans and berries)
- laal maas (a spicy mutton curry)
- sweets such as ghevar, which is seasonal

Much of the food is rich in ghee and can be spicy. Vegetarian food is everywhere and Jain meals are common. Halal food is available in some restaurants.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Which terminal your flight uses at Jaipur International Airport, and current international routes
- Flight, train and road connections and journey times if you travel via Delhi
- Jaipur Metro coverage and any extensions
- Opening days and ticketing rules at monuments and museums
- Heatwave and dust advisories in summer
- Festival dates during your stay
