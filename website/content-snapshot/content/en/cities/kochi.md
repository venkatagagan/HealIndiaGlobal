# Kochi city guide

Kochi (also known as Cochin) is a port city on the coast of Kerala. It spreads across the mainland (Ernakulam) and a group of islands and peninsulas, including Fort Kochi, and ferries are a normal way to get around. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | Kerala |
| Main airport | Cochin International Airport (COK), Nedumbassery |
| Local language(s) | Malayalam. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |

## Arriving by air

Cochin International Airport is at Nedumbassery, north-east of the city. By road, allow roughly 45 minutes to over an hour to central Ernakulam. Fort Kochi takes longer.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Buses and metro feeder services.** Buses link the airport with the city. Feeder buses have connected the airport with Aluva, the nearest Kochi Metro station. These options suit you only if you are walking comfortably and carrying little luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** Check which terminal your flight uses. Confirm current airport bus and metro feeder services and their timings before you rely on them.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | December–February | Warm and drier, with lower humidity. These are the most comfortable months |
| Summer | March–May | Hot and very humid, with pre-monsoon showers from around April |
| Southwest monsoon | June–September | Very heavy rain, especially in June and July. Flooding, waterlogging and rough seas are possible |
| Northeast monsoon | October–November | Afternoon and evening thunderstorms and showers |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Humidity.** Kochi is humid all year. Drink fluids as your care team advises, and keep wound dressings clean and dry. Ask your nurse how to manage them in humid weather.
- **Monsoon.** Follow the warnings from the India Meteorological Department (IMD) and the Kerala State Disaster Management Authority. Keep your plans flexible.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue and other mosquito-borne illnesses rises during and after the rains. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.

## Getting around

- **Kochi Metro.** A single line runs from Aluva through Ernakulam towards Tripunithura.
- **Kochi Water Metro and public ferries.** Water Metro boats link the mainland with nearby islands, and public ferries also run between Ernakulam and Fort Kochi. Boarding involves steps and gangways, so ask for help.
- **App-based cabs and auto-rickshaws.** Cabs are the most practical option for most patients. Autos are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Traffic.** Roads are often narrow, and congestion builds at peak times.
- **Walking.** Pavements are uneven and often wet in the rainy months, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Fort Kochi waterfront.** A flat seaside walkway beside the Chinese fishing nets. It is quieter in the morning.
- **Mattancherry.** A historic area with the Mattancherry Palace (also called the Dutch Palace) and the Paradesi Synagogue. The streets are mostly flat but narrow and busy with shops.
- **Marine Drive walkway, Ernakulam.** A promenade along the backwaters with views of the harbour, popular in the evening.
- **Hill Palace Museum, Tripunithura.** A former royal palace complex, now a museum. The grounds slope and there are some steps.
- **A gentle backwater boat trip, later in recovery.** Go only with your doctor's agreement. Short day cruises run around Kochi, and Alappuzha (Alleppey) is one to two hours away by road. Avoid overnight houseboat stays early in your recovery, because you would be far from medical help.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

> **Important:** Kerala is known for Ayurveda. Talk to your treating doctor before you have any Ayurvedic massage, oil treatment or herbal remedy during recovery. Some treatments can affect wound healing, and some herbal products can interact with your medicines.

Leave some trips until later in your recovery. These include hill stations such as Munnar, which mean several hours on winding mountain roads, long houseboat trips and crowded festival events.

## Food

Kerala food uses coconut, curry leaves and spices. Typical dishes include:

- fish curries and seafood such as karimeen (pearl spot)
- appam with stew
- puttu with kadala (chickpea) curry
- parotta
- sadya, a vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf, especially during the Onam festival

Malabar-style biryani is also common. Vegetarian and halal food are widely available. Jain meals are less common, so ask in advance.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Which terminal your flight uses at Cochin International Airport
- Airport bus and metro feeder services, and their timings
- Kochi Metro and Kochi Water Metro routes and timings
- Monsoon warnings from the IMD and the Kerala State Disaster Management Authority
- Opening days at the Mattancherry Palace, the Paradesi Synagogue and the Hill Palace Museum
