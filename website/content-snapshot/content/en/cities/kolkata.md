# Kolkata city guide

Kolkata, the capital of West Bengal, is a densely populated city on the Hooghly river. Hospitals are spread across the city, including in the eastern areas of Salt Lake, New Town and along the Eastern Metropolitan Bypass. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | West Bengal |
| Main airport | Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport (CCU), Dum Dum |
| Local language(s) | Bengali (Bangla); Hindi is widely understood. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |
| Time zone | India Standard Time (UTC+5:30) |
| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport is at Dum Dum, in the north-east of the city. By road, allow roughly 30 minutes to over an hour, depending on where you are going and the traffic.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use the prepaid counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Kolkata Metro.** The metro network has been extended towards the airport. It suits you only if you are walking comfortably and carrying little luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** Check whether the airport metro station is open and how it connects to the terminal. Confirm the terminal arrangements for your flight.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | December–February | Cool, dry and pleasant, with misty mornings. These are the most comfortable months |
| Pre-monsoon summer | March–May | Hot and very humid. Sudden evening thunderstorms with strong winds (known locally as Kalbaishakhi, or nor'westers) are common |
| Monsoon | June–September | Heavy rain. Streets get waterlogged and traffic is disrupted |
| Autumn | October–November | Warm and gradually drier. The Durga Puja festival, usually in September or October, brings very large crowds and traffic restrictions for several days |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Humidity.** Drink fluids as your care team advises, and keep wound dressings clean and dry.
- **Storms and cyclones.** Cyclones in the Bay of Bengal can affect the city, mainly before and after the monsoon. Follow the warnings from the India Meteorological Department (IMD) and keep your plans flexible.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.
- **Floodwater.** Do not walk through waterlogged streets, which can hide hazards and carry infections.

## Getting around

- **Kolkata Metro.** India's oldest metro system. It includes the north–south Blue Line and several newer lines, such as the East–West (Green) Line, which runs under the Hooghly to Howrah. More sections are being built.
- **Taxis and app-based cabs.** Yellow taxis are common; ask for the meter. App-based cabs are the most practical option for most patients.
- **Auto-rickshaws.** These often run as shared autos on fixed routes. That can be confusing for visitors, and autos are not suitable after surgery.
- **Ferries.** Ferries cross the Hooghly, but boarding can involve steps and uneven jetties.
- **Traffic.** Roads are congested, and waterlogging during heavy rain slows everything down.
- **Walking.** Pavements are crowded and uneven, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Victoria Memorial.** A marble memorial hall and museum set in large, mostly flat gardens.
- **Prinsep Ghat and the riverside.** A riverside promenade with a colonial-era memorial and views of the Vidyasagar Setu bridge. It is pleasant in the evening but can be crowded at weekends.
- **Eco Park, New Town.** A large urban park with lakes, themed gardens and flat paths. Some areas need separate tickets.
- **Rabindra Sarobar.** A lake in south Kolkata with paths around it, popular with morning walkers.
- **Indian Museum.** A large museum founded in 1814. Its extensive galleries mean long walks and some stairs, so take breaks.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some outings until later in your recovery. These include visiting the night-time crowds at Durga Puja, busy markets such as New Market and Burrabazar, and long trips such as the Sundarbans, which mean a long road and boat journey.

## Food

Bengali cooking is built around rice and fish. You will find rohu and, in season, hilsa (ilish), and prawn malai curry. Other dishes include shukto (mixed vegetables) and sweets such as mishti doi (sweetened yoghurt), rosogolla and sandesh. Kolkata biryani, often made with potato and egg, kathi rolls and Indo-Chinese food are also popular. Vegetarian food is available, but many Bengali dishes include fish, so ask. Halal food is widely available, and some vegetarian restaurants offer Jain meals.

If you have diabetes, take care with sweets and follow your care team's advice. Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Whether the airport metro station is open and how it connects to the terminal
- Kolkata Metro lines currently running and new sections
- Durga Puja dates (they change every year) and related traffic restrictions
- Cyclone and heavy rain warnings
- Opening days and rules at the Victoria Memorial, the Indian Museum and Eco Park
