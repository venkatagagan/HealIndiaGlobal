# Mumbai city guide

Mumbai is a dense, crowded coastal city. Hospitals are spread from South Mumbai through the western and eastern suburbs to Navi Mumbai and Thane, and journeys that look short on a map can take a long time. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | Maharashtra |
| Main airport | Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM). Navi Mumbai International Airport is a newer second airport |
| Local language(s) | Marathi; Hindi is widely spoken. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |
| Time zone | India Standard Time (UTC+5:30) |
| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport is in the northern suburbs. By road, allow roughly 20 minutes to an hour to hospitals in the western suburbs. South Mumbai, Navi Mumbai and Thane often take an hour or more, and much longer in heavy traffic or monsoon rain. Navi Mumbai International Airport is across the harbour, so journey times from it differ. Ask your coordinator.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long flight or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Official prepaid taxi counters or app-based cabs.** Use counters inside the terminal or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.
3. **Mumbai Metro.** Line 3, the underground Aqua Line, has stations serving the main airport's terminals. The metro suits you only if you are walking comfortably and carrying little luggage.

> **Check before you travel:** Check which airport (Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International or Navi Mumbai International) and which terminal your flight uses. International flights at the main airport have used Terminal 2, but allocations change. Confirm metro stations and operating hours before you rely on them.

## Climate by season

| Season | Approx. months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | December–February | Warm days, milder nights and lower humidity. These are the most comfortable months |
| Summer | March–May | Hot and humid |
| Monsoon | June–September | Very heavy rain. Low-lying areas flood, roads get waterlogged, and traffic and trains are disrupted |
| Post-monsoon | October–November | Hot and humid again as the rains end, with occasional late showers |

What this means for your recovery:

- **Humidity and heat.** Drink fluids as your care team advises. Keep wound dressings clean and dry, and ask your nurse how to manage them in humid weather.
- **Monsoon.** Leave extra time for appointments, because roads can flood quickly and flights may be delayed. Do not walk through floodwater, which can carry infections.
- **Mosquitoes.** The risk of dengue and malaria rises during and after the monsoon. Use repellent and cover your arms and legs.

## Getting around

- **App-based cabs and black-and-yellow taxis.** These are the most practical options for most patients. Taxis are metered; check that the meter is running.
- **Auto-rickshaws.** They operate in the suburbs but not in South Mumbai. They are bumpy and open-sided, so they are not ideal after surgery.
- **Suburban trains.** They are extremely crowded at most times of day and are not suitable while you are recovering.
- **Mumbai Metro.** Several lines are open and more are being built. Construction can also cause road diversions.
- **Traffic.** Traffic is heavy for much of the day. Travel outside peak hours where you can.
- **Walking.** Pavements are often narrow, uneven or crowded, and step-free routes are rare. After surgery, use hospital transport or a car arranged by your coordinator.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Only go out once your doctor agrees, keep trips short, avoid crowds and heat, and take your companion.

- **Marine Drive.** A long, flat seafront promenade in South Mumbai. It is pleasant in the early morning or evening but has little shade, and waves can splash over the sea wall in the monsoon.
- **Gateway of India and Colaba.** A harbour-front monument and the streets around it. They are crowded in the evenings and at weekends, so keep visits short.
- **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya.** A large museum in the Fort area, with indoor galleries and gardens. The galleries are on several floors, so ask about lifts.
- **Bandra seafront promenades.** Bandstand and Carter Road have flat walkways along the sea. They are busy in the evenings.
- **Juhu Beach.** A wide beach that is busy around sunset. Soft sand is tiring to walk on, so stay on firm ground, and do not swim.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go.

Leave some trips until later in your recovery. These include the Elephanta Caves (a ferry ride followed by many steps), the hilly trails of Sanjay Gandhi National Park, and crowded festival events such as the Ganesh Chaturthi processions.

## Food

Mumbai's food is very varied. You will find:

- Maharashtrian dishes such as poha, misal pav and vada pav
- coastal seafood in Malvani and Konkani styles
- Gujarati vegetarian meals
- Parsi and Irani café food
- street snacks such as pav bhaji and bhel puri

Vegetarian food is everywhere. Halal food is widely available, and many restaurants serve Jain meals.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Which airport (Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International or Navi Mumbai International) and which terminal your flight uses
- Metro stations at the airport terminals, and current metro routes and hours
- Whether auto-rickshaws operate where you are staying
- Monsoon weather warnings and disruption to roads, trains and flights
- Opening days and rules at museums, promenades and beaches
