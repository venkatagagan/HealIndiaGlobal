# Visakhapatnam city guide

Visakhapatnam, often called Vizag, is a port city on the Bay of Bengal in Andhra Pradesh, set between the Eastern Ghats hills and the sea. It is the state's largest city and a growing centre for healthcare. This guide gives practical notes for patients and companions. Details marked **Check before you travel** can change, so confirm them before you rely on them.

## At a glance

| Topic | Details |
|---|---|
| State/territory | Andhra Pradesh |
| Main airport | Visakhapatnam International Airport (VTZ) |
| Local language(s) | Telugu. English is widely used in hospitals and hotels |
| Time zone | India Standard Time (UTC+5:30) |
| Emergency number | 112 (national emergency number) |

## Arriving by air

Visakhapatnam International Airport is in the south-west of the city. By road, allow roughly 20 to 40 minutes to the central areas and the beach road, longer at peak times. Many international journeys connect through a larger hub such as Hyderabad, Chennai, Bengaluru, Delhi or Mumbai, so plan for a domestic connection.

Ways to reach your hospital or accommodation:

1. **Airport pickup arranged through your coordinator or hospital.** This is the simplest option after a long journey or if you have mobility needs. Your coordinator will share the driver's details before you land.
2. **Prepaid taxis or app-based cabs.** Use the official counters or an app, and board at the marked pick-up points.

> **Check before you travel:** Check which international and domestic routes currently serve Visakhapatnam, and allow enough time between connecting flights, including for immigration and baggage at your first Indian airport. A new airport at Bhogapuram, north of the city, is planned; confirm which airport your flight uses.

## Climate by season

| Season | Months | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Summer | March to June | Hot and humid; the sea breeze helps, but avoid the midday sun |
| South-west monsoon | June to September | Humid, with regular rain |
| North-east monsoon and cyclone season | October to December | Heavy rain and occasional cyclones; follow weather warnings closely |
| Winter | December to February | The most comfortable months: warm days and pleasant evenings |

## Getting around

- **Cabs and autorickshaws** are the usual way to get around. App-based cabs and autos are widely available; agree the fare or use the meter for street autos.
- **Hospital transport** is often available for appointments; ask your coordinator.
- The city is hilly in places and spread along the coast, so short distances can take longer than expected.

## Recovery-friendly outings

Choose short, low-effort outings that your care team approves.

- **Ramakrishna (RK) Beach and the beach road.** A level promenade for a gentle evening walk. Do not swim: currents are strong.
- **Kailasagiri.** A hilltop park with views over the city and sea, reached by road or ropeway. Check that the ropeway is running and avoid it if you are unsteady.
- **INS Kursura Submarine Museum.** A decommissioned submarine on the beach road. Inside it is narrow with ladders, so go only when you move easily.

Opening days, hours, entry rules and fees change, so check before you go. Leave longer trips, such as the Araku Valley or the Borra Caves, until later in your recovery: they involve long drives and walking.

## Food

Andhra food is known for being spicy, and as a coastal city Visakhapatnam serves a lot of seafood. Rice with dal, sambar, rasam, pickles and curd is a typical meal, and biryani is popular. Ask for food to be made mild if you prefer. Vegetarian food is widely available and halal food is available in many areas.

Follow the diet your care team gives you and avoid street food and raw seafood while you recover. The [in-country guide](../in-country-guide.md) covers food and water safety.

## Things to check before you travel

- Current flight routes and which airport your flight uses
- Weather and cyclone warnings from IMD, especially from October to December
- Whether the Kailasagiri ropeway and museums are open on your dates
- Dress codes and access rules at temples and other places of worship
