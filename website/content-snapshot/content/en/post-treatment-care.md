# Post-treatment care

Recovery does not end when you leave the hospital. This guide covers what to collect before discharge, how to look after yourself in India, how to prepare for the flight home and how to set up follow-up care in your own country. Your treating team's instructions always come first. The procedure guides add advice for specific treatments.

> **Emergency:** Get urgent medical help if you have chest pain, difficulty breathing, sudden weakness or numbness, confusion, heavy bleeding, a high fever or a fever with shivering, or pain and swelling in one leg. In India, call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department. At home, call your local emergency number.

## Before you leave hospital: your discharge pack

Ask for these documents before discharge, in English or your preferred language, and upload them to the app (Documents):

- [ ] **Discharge summary.** Your diagnosis, the procedure done and its date, the treating doctors, the main findings and any complications.
- [ ] **Operation or procedure notes**, if you had surgery.
- [ ] **Implant details**, if you received an implant, such as a joint, valve, stent, lens or dental implant: the make, model and size, and an implant card if the hospital provides one.
- [ ] **Copies of key test results and imaging**, including digital image files where available.
- [ ] **Pathology or biopsy results.** Some results arrive after discharge, so ask how and when you will receive them.
- [ ] **Medicine list.** The generic name, strength, dose, timing and duration of each medicine, what it is for, and any medicines you should stop.
- [ ] **Care instructions.** Wound care, when stitches or clips come out, and limits on activity and diet.
- [ ] **Warning signs**, and who to contact at any hour.
- [ ] **Follow-up plan.** Reviews in India before you fly, teleconsultation dates, and the tests needed at home and when.
- [ ] **A fitness-to-fly letter or medical certificate**, if your airline, insurer or employer needs one.
- [ ] **Final bill and receipts**, which you will need for any insurance claim.

> **Tip:** Ask your treating team to write every medicine by its generic name, as well as the brand name. This helps pharmacies at home find an equivalent.

## Recovering in India before you fly

- **Follow your plan.** Take medicines on time, do the walking, breathing or physiotherapy exercises you were shown, look after your wound and follow your diet.
- **Attend every review.** Wound checks, stitch removal, scans and blood tests are often scheduled before you are cleared to fly.
- **Rest and avoid infection.** Avoid crowds and the heat, follow the food and water advice in the [in-country guide](in-country-guide.md), and do not swim until your doctor says your wounds have healed.
- **Go out only when your doctor agrees.** The city guides suggest short, low-effort outings.
- **Keep your coordinator informed.** If your discharge or flight date changes, your accommodation, transport and possibly your visa need to change too.

## Flying home

- **Fit to fly.** Fly only when your treating doctor confirms you are fit to fly. Ask for a letter if your airline needs one. Some airlines also ask for their own medical clearance form (often called a MEDIF).
- **Help at the airport.** Book a wheelchair or other assistance with your airline in advance, and ask about seating that suits your recovery.
- **Blood clots.** Long journeys after treatment raise the risk of blood clots in the legs. Ask your doctor whether you need compression stockings or blood-thinning medicine. During the flight, move your legs, walk when it is safe, drink water and avoid alcohol and sleeping tablets.
- **Medicines.** Carry your medicines in your hand luggage, with your prescriptions and the medicine list. If you use injections or needles, carry a doctor's letter. Ask your doctor or pharmacist how to adjust medicine times across time zones.
- **Implants and devices.** Carry your implant card or a letter for airport security.
- **Supplies.** If you have dressings, a catheter or a drain, carry enough supplies for the whole journey plus delays.
- **Oxygen.** If you need medical oxygen during the flight, arrange it with the airline well before you fly. Each airline has its own rules.
- **Luggage.** Let your companion carry the bags. Avoid lifting heavy luggage until your doctor says it is safe.
- **Insurance.** Check that your travel insurance covers the journey home.

## Follow-up care at home

- **See your local doctor soon after you return,** or as your treating team advises. Bring your discharge summary and medicine list. Ideally, book this appointment before you leave for India.
- **Specialist care.** Arrange the specialist follow-up, physiotherapy or rehabilitation your treating team recommended.
- **Tests.** Have the blood tests, scans or other checks you were told you need. If your doctor in India asked to see the results, upload them to the app.
- **Teleconsultations.** Where your treating doctor offers video or phone follow-up, request it in the app. Any fee is shown before you confirm.
- **Recovery diary.** Note your temperature, pain levels, how your wound looks (photos help), new symptoms and questions. This makes consultations more useful.
- **Medicines.** Get repeat prescriptions in good time. Do not stop or change a medicine without medical advice. Some countries limit the medicines you can bring in, so check your home country's rules before buying extra medicines in India.
- **Stitches and wound care.** If stitches or clips were not removed in India, arrange removal with your local doctor or nurse at the time your treating team advised.

## Warning signs: when to get urgent help

Get urgent medical help, and do not wait for a teleconsultation, if you notice any of these:

- fever, chills or shivering
- redness, swelling, warmth, pus or a bad smell around a wound, or a wound that opens
- bleeding that does not stop
- chest pain, a racing heartbeat or difficulty breathing
- pain, swelling, warmth or redness in one calf or leg
- a sudden severe headache, weakness, numbness, confusion or difficulty speaking
- severe or worsening pain that your medicines do not control
- vomiting, or being unable to keep fluids down
- passing very little urine, or very dark urine
- any sign of infection if your immunity is low, for example after a transplant or during chemotherapy

Call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department, and take your discharge summary and medicine list. When you are safe, tell your treating hospital through the app so it can share information with your local doctors.

## Looking after your wellbeing

Recovery takes time and energy. It is common to feel tired, low or worried after major treatment, especially far from home. Tell your doctor if your mood stays low or you feel anxious; support is available. Ask your doctor when you can safely return to work, driving, exercise, sport and sexual activity. In some countries, rules on driving after surgery or illness also apply.

## Keeping your records

- Keep all your documents together in the app, and keep paper copies at home.
- Share them securely with your local doctors.
- Carry your implant card or medical device card in your wallet.
- Keep the final bill and receipts until any insurance claim is settled.

## Sharing feedback

After your treatment you can rate and review your hospital in the app. Reviews are checked before they are published. Honest feedback helps other patients, and it helps hospitals improve.
