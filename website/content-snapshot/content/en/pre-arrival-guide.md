# Pre-arrival guide

This guide takes you from about eight weeks before you travel to the day you land in India. Your own timeline may be longer or shorter. Some steps, such as getting a visa, cannot be rushed, so start early. You can follow your progress in the checklist in your Trips dashboard, which covers:

- treatment booked
- visa approved
- flight booked
- travel insurance
- airport pickup
- accommodation booked
- documents uploaded

> **Emergency:** If your condition gets worse while you are planning, get medical care where you are. Do not travel if a doctor advises against it, and tell your coordinator so the hospital can review your plans.

## Timeline at a glance

| When | Main tasks |
|---|---|
| About 8 weeks before | Medical records, quotes, passport check, travel-health appointment |
| 6 to 7 weeks before | Book treatment, pay the deposit, get the hospital letter, decide who travels with you |
| 4 to 6 weeks before | Apply for visas, book flights and accommodation, buy travel insurance |
| 2 to 3 weeks before | Airport pickup, translator, money, phone, medicines, plans for your return home |
| Final week | Confirm everything, follow pre-treatment instructions, pack |
| Arrival day | Immigration, meet your driver, check in, rest |

## About 8 weeks before (or as soon as you start planning)

### Medical records

- [ ] Gather your recent records:
  - letters from your doctors
  - test results
  - imaging reports and the images themselves (DICOM files from a CD or USB drive, if you can get them)
  - pathology and biopsy reports
  - operation notes and discharge summaries
- [ ] Write a list of your medicines. For each one, note the generic name, strength, how often you take it and what it is for. Add any allergies.
- [ ] Upload your records in the app (Documents). PDF, JPG, PNG, WEBP, HEIC and DICOM files are accepted.
- [ ] Ask your local doctor for a summary or referral letter, and whether they can support your follow-up when you return.
- [ ] If your records are not in English, ask your coordinator whether the hospital needs translations.

### Quotes and consultations

- [ ] Complete the screening questionnaire (AI is optional), or search for hospitals and doctors yourself.
- [ ] Request quotes from the hospitals you choose, and compare what each one includes.
- [ ] Book a video consultation if the doctor offers one. Read the procedure guide for your treatment so you can prepare your questions.

### Passport and health

- [ ] Check that your passport will be valid for long enough and has blank pages. Passports usually need at least six months' validity from your arrival date, but confirm the current rule on the official e-Visa website.
- [ ] Book a travel-health appointment with your doctor or a travel clinic for advice on vaccines and preventive measures.
- [ ] Ask your doctor whether you are fit to travel for treatment.

> **Check before you travel:** India may require a valid yellow fever vaccination certificate if you arrive from, or have travelled through, a country with a risk of yellow fever. Travellers from some countries may also need proof of polio vaccination. Check the current rules with a travel clinic or official Indian government sources well before you fly.

## 6 to 7 weeks before

### Book your treatment

- [ ] Accept the quote that suits you, then choose your admission date, package and payment option.
- [ ] Read the cancellation and refund terms before you pay. See [Payments and refunds](payments-and-refunds.md).
- [ ] Pay the deposit, or the full amount. Your booking is confirmed once at least the deposit has been paid, and your Trips checklist starts.

### Hospital letter for your visa

- [ ] Your coordinator asks the hospital for the letter you need for your visa application.
- [ ] Check that every name and passport number in the letter matches the passports exactly, for you and for each companion.

### Decide who travels with you

- [ ] For major treatment, plan to bring at least one companion.
- [ ] Add each companion to your profile with their full name, relationship, nationality, date of birth and passport details. These are needed for hospital letters and attendant visas.
- [ ] Some treatments, such as organ and bone marrow transplants, need a dedicated carer and sometimes a donor. Discuss this with your coordinator early, because donors need extra documents and approvals.

## 4 to 6 weeks before

### Apply for your visas

Most patients use India's **e-Medical visa**. Companions usually apply for the **e-Medical Attendant visa**, which is linked to the patient's e-Medical visa. The Government of India sets the number of attendants allowed and who may apply.

- [ ] Apply only on the official Government of India e-Visa website. Unofficial sites may charge extra.
- [ ] Prepare the documents the website asks for. These usually include a photograph, a scan of your passport's photo page and the hospital letter.
- [ ] Apply as early as the official application window allows. Do not book non-refundable flights until your visa is approved.
- [ ] Tell your coordinator when you apply and when you receive a decision, so your Trips checklist stays up to date.
- [ ] Print or save your visa approval and keep it with your passport.
- [ ] e-Visa holders must enter India through designated airports or seaports. Check that your arrival airport is on the official list.

If you cannot get an e-Visa, ask the Indian embassy or consulate in your country about a regular medical visa.

> **Check before you travel:** Visa types, eligibility by nationality, required documents, fees, processing times, application windows and permitted entry points all change. Confirm everything on the official e-Visa website (https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/) before you apply. We cannot guarantee a visa; only the Government of India decides.

### Flights

- [ ] Book flights to the airport closest to your hospital. The city guides list the main airports.
- [ ] Choose flexible or changeable fares, and leave extra time before your return flight, because your doctor decides when you are fit to fly.
- [ ] Ask your airline about help at the airport (for example a wheelchair), seating, and its rules for medical conditions and equipment such as oxygen or CPAP machines.
- [ ] Share your flight details with your coordinator so your pickup can be arranged.

### Accommodation

- [ ] Request accommodation in the app: a hospital guest room, service apartment, guest house or hotel.
- [ ] Think about the distance to the hospital, lifts and step-free access, a kitchen for special diets, laundry, air conditioning and space for your companion.
- [ ] Ask about flexible dates in case you need to stay longer.

### Travel insurance

- [ ] Standard travel insurance often excludes planned treatment abroad. Look for cover suitable for a medical trip and read the policy carefully.
- [ ] Check what the policy covers:
  - complications
  - a longer stay
  - medical repatriation
  - trip cancellation
  - your companions
- [ ] Declare your medical conditions honestly, or the policy may not pay.
- [ ] Keep the policy number and the insurer's emergency assistance number on your phone. You can add your insurance details to your profile.

## 2 to 3 weeks before

### Airport pickup and translator

- [ ] Book airport pickup in the app with your flight number and arrival time. Tell your coordinator about any mobility needs or extra luggage.
- [ ] Request a translator for important appointments, especially your first consultation, consent discussions, the day of your procedure and discharge.

### Money and cards

- [ ] Tell your bank and card issuer that you are travelling to India. Check their fees for using your card and withdrawing cash abroad.
- [ ] Carry two payment cards if you can, ideally on different card networks, and keep them separately.
- [ ] Plan to get some Indian rupees (INR) for small purchases after you arrive, from an airport exchange counter, a bank or an ATM.
- [ ] Pay for your treatment through the app or patient portal, not in cash.
- [ ] When a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, choosing rupees usually avoids an extra conversion charge by the merchant.

> **Check before you travel:** India has rules on how much foreign currency you can bring in before you must declare it, and restrictions on carrying Indian rupees into or out of the country. Check the current limits with Indian Customs (CBIC) or the Reserve Bank of India before you travel. Some international visitors can use UPI (India's mobile payment system) through special services for foreign travellers; check what is available to you.

### Phone and internet

- [ ] Decide how you will stay connected:
  - **International roaming.** Check the cost with your mobile provider.
  - **eSIM.** If your phone supports it, an international eSIM bought before you travel can work as soon as you land.
  - **Indian prepaid SIM.** You can buy one at airport counters or official stores. You will need your passport, visa and usually a photograph and a local address, such as your hotel. Activation can take some hours.
- [ ] Make sure your phone is unlocked if you plan to use an Indian SIM.
- [ ] Install the Heal India Global app, turn on notifications and sign in. Also install a maps app, a translation app and a ride-hailing app.

### Medicines

- [ ] Bring enough of your regular medicines for the whole trip, plus extra in case of delays.
- [ ] Keep them in their original packaging, in your hand luggage, with your prescriptions or a doctor's letter that lists the generic names.
- [ ] Some medicines, such as strong painkillers and some sleeping or anxiety medicines, are controlled in India or in countries you pass through. Check the rules before you travel.
- [ ] Ask your treating team which medicines, if any, you should stop before your procedure, and when. This includes blood thinners, diabetes medicines and herbal supplements. Stop medicines only when your treating team tells you to.

### Plan for your return

- [ ] Arrange a follow-up appointment with your local doctor for after you return.
- [ ] Arrange help at home, time off work and any equipment you may need, for example a walking frame or a shower chair.
- [ ] Ask your treating team what physiotherapy or follow-up tests you will need at home, so you can book them in advance.

## Final week

- [ ] Check your itinerary in the app: admission date, pickup time, accommodation and translator bookings.
- [ ] Save copies of your documents on your phone, and also print them:
  - passport
  - visa approval
  - hospital letter
  - booking confirmation
  - insurance documents
  - medical records
  - medicine list
  - emergency contacts
- [ ] Follow your treating team's instructions before treatment, such as fasting, stopping or adjusting medicines, or stopping smoking.
- [ ] Share your itinerary and the hospital's contact details with family at home.
- [ ] If you feel unwell before you fly, for example with a fever, an infection or new symptoms, tell your coordinator and the hospital. They may need to change your dates.

## Packing list

### Documents (in your hand luggage)

- [ ] Passport, plus photocopies or photos of it
- [ ] Visa approval and the hospital letter
- [ ] Booking confirmation and insurance documents
- [ ] Medical records, imaging files and your medicine list with prescriptions
- [ ] Emergency contacts and payment cards

### Health items

- [ ] Medicines in their original packaging, in hand luggage
- [ ] Compression stockings, if your doctor advises them
- [ ] Glasses, contact lens supplies and hearing-aid batteries
- [ ] Any medical devices you use, after checking your airline's rules
- [ ] Thermometer, hand sanitiser, face masks, insect repellent and sunscreen

### Clothing

- [ ] Loose, comfortable clothes that are easy to put on, including front-opening tops if you are having chest, heart or shoulder surgery
- [ ] Loose trousers or shorts that fit over dressings or braces, and slip-on shoes with good grip
- [ ] A light layer for air-conditioned rooms, and warmer clothes for north India in winter
- [ ] A light rain jacket during the monsoon months
- [ ] Modest clothing for visiting religious sites

### Practical items

- [ ] Phone, charger and a power bank (power banks must go in hand luggage)
- [ ] Plug adapters: India uses type C, D and M sockets at 230 volts
- [ ] Earplugs, an eye mask, a travel pillow and a reusable water bottle
- [ ] Snacks that suit any special diet, for the journey

> **Tip:** Pack your medicines, documents and a change of clothes in your hand luggage, in case your checked bag is delayed.

## Arrival day

1. **During the flight.** Move your legs, walk when it is safe, drink water and avoid alcohol. Follow any advice your doctor gave you about blood clots.
2. **Immigration.** Have your passport, your visa approval and the hospital letter ready. Your fingerprints and photograph may be taken.
3. **Customs.** Declare anything you are required to declare, including currency above the permitted limits. Keep your medicine prescriptions handy.
4. **Money and SIM.** Exchange a small amount of money and set up your phone if you have not done so already.
5. **Meet your driver.** Check the driver's name and vehicle against the details your coordinator sent, and message your coordinator to say you have landed. Do not accept rides from people who approach you in the terminal.
6. **Check in.** Your accommodation will ask for your passport and visa, because it must record foreign guests.
7. **Rest.** Drink water, eat something light and go to bed early. Your coordinator will confirm your first hospital appointment.

> **Check before you travel:** Arrival formalities, such as arrival or health declaration forms, can change. Check with your airline and official Indian government sources shortly before you fly.
