# Hair transplant

This page explains hair transplant surgery, what the days in India usually look like, and what to expect afterwards. It is general information to help you decide what to ask at your consultation.

## What it is

A hair transplant moves hair follicles from an area where hair grows densely to areas that are thinning or bald. The donor area is usually the back and sides of the scalp. The procedure is done under local anaesthesia, and it is often a long day of several hours. You go back to your accommodation the same day.

There are two main techniques:

- **FUE (follicular unit extraction):** small groups of follicles are removed one at a time with a tiny punch. This leaves small dot-like scars that are usually hidden by surrounding hair.
- **FUT (follicular unit transplantation, or the "strip" method):** a thin strip of scalp is removed from the donor area and divided into small grafts. This leaves a fine line scar.

The grafts are then placed into tiny openings made in the thinning area.

## Why doctors may recommend it

Doctors may suggest a transplant for stable, patterned hair loss, such as male- or female-pattern hair loss, when there is enough healthy donor hair. It is not suitable for every type of hair loss. Hair loss caused by thyroid problems, low iron, some medicines or conditions such as alopecia areata usually needs medical treatment instead. Your doctor may also suggest medical treatment before or alongside surgery.

It helps to have realistic expectations:

- A transplant moves your existing hair. It does not create new follicles.
- Hair loss may continue in areas that were not treated.
- Some people need more than one session.

A consultation with a qualified doctor, such as a dermatologist or plastic surgeon, should assess whether you are suitable. Ask who will carry out each part of the procedure.

## Records to share for a quote

- Clear photos of your scalp from the front, top, crown, both sides and back, taken in good light with dry hair
- The history of your hair loss: when it started, whether it runs in your family, and the treatments you have tried
- Your medical conditions, such as bleeding disorders, diabetes, heart problems or a tendency to raised scars (keloids)
- Your current medicines and supplements, especially blood thinners, and any allergies, including to local anaesthetics
- Details of any previous hair transplant

## Tests usually done before treatment

- An examination of the scalp, sometimes with a magnifying scope, and an assessment of the donor area
- Blood tests, often including a blood count, blood sugar and clotting tests, and commonly screening for infections such as HIV and hepatitis B and C
- Sometimes tests to look for other causes of hair loss, such as thyroid function or iron levels
- A blood pressure check and a general health review

Your hospital will tell you which tests you need; some may be repeated in India.

## Typical time in hospital and in India

| Stage | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital stay | None (day procedure) | Often a long day under local anaesthesia |
| Total time in India | About 3–5 days | Consultation, procedure and a check, often including the first hair wash |

These are typical ranges only. Your time varies by patient; your doctor will advise.

## Recovery

### In India

- Swelling of the forehead and around the eyes is common for a few days. Sleeping with your head raised can help.
- Protect the grafts. Do not touch, scratch or rub the treated area. Avoid hats, helmets and tight head coverings unless your clinic says a loose one is fine.
- Wash your hair only in the way your clinic shows you.
- Tiny crusts form around the grafts and fall away over the following days to weeks.
- Avoid direct sun on the scalp, strenuous exercise, swimming and saunas for as long as advised.
- The donor area may feel numb, tight or sore for a while.

### After you return home

- It is common for transplanted hairs to shed in the first weeks. This is expected and does not mean the procedure has failed.
- New growth usually starts gradually after a few months. It can take a year or more to see the final appearance, and results vary from person to person.
- Continue any medical treatment for hair loss that your doctor prescribed.
- Send progress photos to your clinic for follow-up, for example by video consultation.

## Fitness to fly

- **Protecting the grafts:** take care with overhead lockers and headrests. A travel pillow can help you keep your head upright so the treated area does not rub. Avoid tight caps.
- **Blood clots:** long flights carry a general risk of blood clots (deep vein thrombosis, DVT). Walk and stretch regularly.
- **Medicines:** carry your medicines, sprays and aftercare instructions in your hand luggage. Liquids over the airport limit may need a letter.
- **Paperwork:** medical clearance from the airline (often called a MEDIF) is not usually needed after a hair transplant. Ask your doctor if you are unsure.

Your treating doctor confirms when you are fit to fly, usually at a review before discharge or shortly after.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- What is causing my hair loss, and am I a suitable candidate?
- Do you recommend FUE or FUT for me, and why?
- How many grafts do you plan to use, and what area will they cover? Might I need another session later?
- Who will do each step: the doctor, or trained technicians?
- What result is realistic for my hair type and donor area?
- Which medicines do you recommend before or after the procedure?
- What will the donor area look like if I wear my hair short?
- What are the risks, such as infection, bleeding, poor growth, scarring, numbness or cysts?
- When can I wash my hair, exercise, wear a hat and fly?
- How will follow-up work after I go home?

## When to get urgent help

> **Emergency:** Get urgent help if you have:
>
> - bleeding that does not stop with gentle, steady pressure
> - spreading redness, increasing pain, pus or a fever
> - swelling around the eyes that affects your vision
> - signs of an allergic reaction, such as a rash, swelling of the lips or face, or difficulty breathing
> - chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain and swelling in one leg
>
> In India, call 112 or the hospital. At home, call your local emergency number.
