# LASIK laser eye surgery

This page explains LASIK laser vision correction, what to expect if you have it in India, and questions to ask your eye surgeon. It is general information only.

## What it is

LASIK (laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis) is laser surgery that reshapes the clear front surface of the eye, the cornea. It corrects short-sightedness (myopia), long-sightedness (hyperopia) and astigmatism, so you may depend less on glasses or contact lenses.

The surgeon makes a thin, hinged flap in the cornea, uses a laser to reshape the tissue underneath, and then lays the flap back in place. The eye is numbed with drops, and the laser part usually takes a short time for each eye. Both eyes are often treated on the same day, and you go back to your accommodation the same day.

Your clinic may also discuss related procedures, such as surface laser treatment (PRK), SMILE or implantable lenses. These suit different eyes.

## Why doctors may recommend it

Eye surgeons may suggest LASIK for adults whose glasses prescription has been stable for some time and whose eyes are otherwise healthy. Not everyone is suitable. The thickness and shape of the cornea, a very high prescription, dry eye, some eye conditions, pregnancy and some general health conditions may rule LASIK out or make another option better.

Only a full assessment by an eye surgeon can decide whether it is right for you. LASIK does not prevent the age-related need for reading glasses that usually develops in middle age. It is reasonable to ask about alternatives, including carrying on with glasses or contact lenses.

## Records to share for a quote

- Your current glasses or contact-lens prescription, plus older prescriptions if you have them, to show how stable it is
- Reports from previous eye examinations or scans, including corneal mapping (topography) if you have had it
- Details of any eye conditions, eye injuries, previous eye surgery or dry-eye treatment
- Your general health conditions, such as diabetes or autoimmune conditions, your current medicines and any allergies
- Whether you wear contact lenses, and which type (soft, toric or rigid gas-permeable)

## Tests usually done before treatment

- Vision testing and an accurate measurement of your prescription, often using drops that enlarge the pupils
- Corneal mapping (topography or tomography) and corneal thickness measurement (pachymetry)
- Eye pressure measurement
- An assessment of your tear film for dry eye
- An examination of the front and back of the eye, including the retina
- Pupil size measurement in some clinics

Your hospital will tell you which tests you need; some may be repeated in India.

> **Important:** Contact lenses temporarily change the shape of the cornea. You will be asked to stop wearing them for a period before your assessment, and how long depends on the lens type. Your eye clinic will tell you exactly. Plan for this before you travel and bring a pair of glasses. Drops used during the assessment can blur your vision for some hours, so bring sunglasses and do not drive.

## Typical time in hospital and in India

| Stage | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment | Usually 1 day, sometimes 2 | Detailed eye measurements |
| Hospital stay | None (day procedure) | Both eyes are often treated on the same day |
| Check after surgery | Usually the next day | Sometimes a further check before you leave |
| Total time in India | About 3–7 days | Assessment, surgery, next-day check and any later check |

These are typical ranges only. Your time varies by patient; your doctor will advise.

## Recovery

### In India

- For the first few hours, discomfort, watering, a gritty feeling and blurred or hazy vision are common. Rest your eyes and sleep if you can.
- Use your eye drops exactly as prescribed. These may include antibiotic, anti-inflammatory and lubricating drops.
- Do not rub your eyes. Wear the protective shields at night, or whenever you are told to.
- Keep water, soap and make-up out of your eyes. Avoid swimming pools, the sea, hot tubs and dusty or smoky places for as long as your surgeon advises.
- Wear sunglasses outdoors and take regular breaks from screens.
- Do not drive until your surgeon confirms your vision meets driving standards.

### After you return home

- Vision often keeps settling over the following weeks. Some people notice glare, halos around lights at night, or dry eyes for a while.
- Keep using lubricating drops for as long as advised.
- Arrange follow-up checks with a local optometrist or ophthalmologist, and bring your surgical report to them.
- Keep a copy of your LASIK records. If you ever need cataract surgery in the future, your eye surgeon will need these details to choose the right lens.
- Protect your eyes during contact sports and activities where your eyes could be hit.

## Fitness to fly

- **Dry cabin air:** air on planes is dry and can make healing eyes feel uncomfortable. Use lubricating drops during the flight and keep them in your hand luggage.
- **Eye protection:** if you plan to sleep on the plane, ask whether you should wear your eye shields.
- **Blood clots:** long flights carry a general risk of blood clots (deep vein thrombosis, DVT). Walk and stretch regularly, and keep drinking fluids.
- **Paperwork:** medical clearance from the airline (often called a MEDIF) is not usually needed after routine LASIK. If your doctor has any concerns, ask whether you need one.
- **Medicines:** carry your drops, your prescription and a short letter from the clinic.

Your treating doctor confirms when you are fit to fly, usually at a review before discharge or shortly after.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Am I a suitable candidate, and why? Would a different procedure suit my eyes better?
- What vision can I realistically expect? Will I still need glasses for some activities, or for reading as I get older?
- Which technique will you use, including how the flap is created, and why?
- What are the risks and side effects, such as dry eye, glare, halos, or under- or over-correction, and how are they managed?
- If I need a further treatment (an enhancement) later, what happens, and is it covered by my quote?
- How long must I stop wearing contact lenses before the assessment?
- When can I fly, swim, exercise, wear make-up and drive?
- How long will I need eye drops?
- Can I have copies of all my measurements and the treatment record?
- Who do I contact if I have a problem after I return home?

## When to get urgent help

> **Emergency:** Contact your eye surgeon or get urgent eye care if you have:
>
> - eye pain that is getting worse
> - a sudden drop in vision, or vision that gets worse instead of better
> - increasing redness, swelling or discharge
> - an injury or knock to the eye
> - sensitivity to light that is getting worse
>
> In India, call 112 or the hospital. At home, call your local emergency number or go to an urgent eye service.
