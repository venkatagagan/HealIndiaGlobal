# Rhinoplasty (nose surgery)

This page explains rhinoplasty, how long to plan to stay in India, and what recovery is usually like. It is general information to help you prepare for your consultation.

## What it is

Rhinoplasty is surgery that changes the shape or structure of the nose by reshaping its bone and cartilage. It can be:

- **cosmetic**, to change how the nose looks
- **functional**, to help breathing
- **both**

A functional operation is often combined with a septoplasty, which straightens the wall between the nostrils.

The surgeon may use an "open" approach, with a small cut across the strip of skin between the nostrils, or a "closed" approach, with all cuts inside the nose. The operation is usually done under general anaesthesia and takes a few hours. Afterwards you will usually have a splint or tape on the outside of the nose, and sometimes soft splints or packing inside.

## Why doctors may recommend it

Surgeons may suggest rhinoplasty to improve breathing problems caused by the structure of the nose, to correct the shape after an injury, or for cosmetic reasons. The nose should have finished growing first, which is usually by the late teens.

Surgeons usually aim for a natural result that suits your face. No result can be guaranteed, so a consultation should explore your goals, check your health and give you an honest view of what is achievable. You can often have an initial video consultation before you travel.

Ask about alternatives. Breathing problems caused by allergies or swelling may improve with nasal sprays or other medicines. Some clinics offer injectable fillers to change the shape of the nose without surgery, but these have their own risks. Only your doctors can decide whether surgery is right for you.

## Records to share for a quote

- Clear photos of your face from the front, both sides, both three-quarter angles and from below, with a neutral expression, good lighting and no filters
- A short description of what you would like to change, and any breathing problems such as one blocked side or snoring
- Details of previous nose surgery or injuries, with operation notes if available
- Any sinus CT scans or ENT (ear, nose and throat) reports
- Your medical conditions, current medicines and supplements (including blood thinners, fish oil, vitamin E and herbal remedies), any allergies, and whether you smoke

## Tests usually done before treatment

- A consultation and examination of the nose, sometimes with a small camera (nasal endoscopy)
- Standard photographs, and sometimes computer imaging to discuss your goals
- Blood tests, such as a blood count, clotting, blood sugar and infection screening, and an ECG depending on your age and health
- A CT scan of the sinuses if you have breathing or sinus problems
- An anaesthesia assessment

Your hospital will tell you which tests you need; some may be repeated in India.

## Typical time in hospital and in India

| Stage | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital stay | Day case or 1 night | You leave with a splint, and sometimes packing in the nose |
| Total time in India | About 7–10 days | Includes a check to remove the splint and any stitches, usually about a week after surgery |

These are typical ranges only. Your time varies by patient; your doctor will advise.

## Recovery

### In India

- Swelling and bruising around the nose and eyes are common and are often worst in the first few days.
- Your nose will feel blocked. Breathe through your mouth, do not blow your nose, and sneeze with your mouth open.
- Sleep with your head raised. Use cold compresses as advised, but not on the splint.
- Avoid bending over, heavy lifting and strenuous exercise for as long as your surgeon advises.
- Take your medicines and use saline sprays exactly as prescribed.
- Avoid smoking and protect your nose from the sun.

### After you return home

- Most visible bruising fades within a couple of weeks. Swelling goes down more slowly, over months. The final shape, especially at the tip, can take a year or more to settle.
- Avoid contact sports and knocks to the nose for as long as advised.
- If you wear glasses, ask how to stop them resting on your nose while it heals.
- Send follow-up photos to your surgeon as arranged, and find a local ENT or plastic surgeon you could see if needed.
- Some people want or need a second operation (a revision). Ask about the clinic's approach to this before surgery.

## Fitness to fly

- **Pressure changes:** ask your surgeon about flying with a swollen or packed nose. Changes in cabin pressure can cause discomfort, and there is a small risk of a nosebleed.
- **On board:** drink water, use saline spray if advised, and avoid alcohol.
- **Blood clots:** long flights carry a general risk of blood clots (deep vein thrombosis, DVT). Ask about precautions, and walk and stretch regularly.
- **Paperwork:** carry your medicines and discharge letter in your hand luggage. If your surgeon has concerns, the airline may ask for a medical clearance form, often called a MEDIF.

Your treating doctor confirms when you are fit to fly, usually at a review before discharge or shortly after.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- What can realistically be achieved for my nose and face?
- Will the operation also improve my breathing? Do I need a septoplasty?
- Will you use an open or closed approach, and why?
- Will I need cartilage grafts, and where would they come from?
- How often do you perform this operation? Can I see before-and-after photos of patients who agreed to share them?
- What are the risks, such as bleeding, infection, breathing problems, asymmetry or needing a revision?
- How long will the swelling last, and when will I see the final shape?
- When can I fly, exercise and wear glasses?
- What is your policy if a revision is needed?
- How will follow-up work once I am home?

## When to get urgent help

> **Emergency:** Get urgent help if you have:
>
> - a heavy nosebleed that does not stop when you sit forward and apply gentle pressure
> - a fever, or increasing redness, pain or swelling
> - changes in your vision, or swelling around the eye that keeps getting worse
> - difficulty breathing or chest pain
> - pain, swelling or redness in one leg
>
> In India, call 112 or the hospital. At home, call your local emergency number.
