# e-Medical visa overview

Most international patients travel to India for treatment on an **e-Medical visa**, and their companions on an **e-Medical Attendant visa**. This page explains how these visas fit into your plans. It is a general overview, not legal or immigration advice.

> **Check before you travel:** Visa types, eligibility by nationality, required documents, fees, processing times, how long you may stay, the number of entries and the airports and seaports where e-Visa holders may enter all change. Check the current rules on the official Government of India e-Visa website (https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/) before you apply, and apply only there. Unofficial websites may charge extra.

## The two visas

| Visa | Who it is for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| e-Medical visa | The patient travelling for treatment | Usually needs a letter from the Indian hospital that will treat you |
| e-Medical Attendant visa | Family members or companions travelling with the patient | Linked to the patient's e-Medical visa; the Government of India sets how many attendants are allowed and who may apply |

If you cannot use the e-Visa, for example because of your nationality or the length of your treatment, ask the Indian embassy or consulate in your country about a regular medical visa.

## Documents usually requested

The official website lists the documents for your case. They usually include:

- a recent photograph that meets the website's specifications
- a scan of the photo page of your passport
- a letter from the Indian hospital about your treatment
- for attendants, details of the patient's visa application

Make sure every name and passport number matches your passport exactly. A mismatch can delay or stop an application.

## How we help

- Your coordinator asks the hospital for the letter you need and checks the names and passport numbers with you.
- Your Trips checklist reminds you when to apply and records when your visa is approved.
- We do **not** apply for visas on your behalf, charge visa fees or influence decisions. Only the Government of India decides whether to grant a visa.

## Timing tips

- Apply as early as the official application window allows.
- Do not book non-refundable flights until your visa is approved.
- Choose an arrival airport that is on the official list of entry points for e-Visa holders.
- Keep a printed or saved copy of your visa approval with your passport.

## If your plans change

If your treatment takes longer than planned, tell your coordinator and the hospital's international patient desk early. Rules on extending a stay are set by the Indian authorities; the hospital can tell you which documents it can provide.
